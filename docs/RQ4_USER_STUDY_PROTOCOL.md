# RQ4 owner-facing explanation study protocol

**Status:** planned; not yet conducted. The 1,800 prepared scenario cases and their constant `human_rating=YES` field are not participant observations.

## Research question

Do concise Bangla explanations of stock and replenishment recommendations improve SME owners' task accuracy, confidence and perceived usability compared with a numeric recommendation card alone, without creating an unacceptable increase in decision time?

## Design

Use a counterbalanced within-participant comparison:

- **Condition A:** the recommendation card with action, suggested quantity, stock position, reorder point and budget.
- **Condition B:** the same card plus a short Bangla explanation that states the evidence, calculation, pack rounding, budget constraint and supplier lead time.

Use matched but non-identical scenarios across conditions. Randomise condition order and scenario order. Balance tasks across sufficient-stock, reorder, pending-receipt and budget-insufficient cases. Pilot the task wording before the main study.

## Participants and sample size

Recruit owners or managers who make purchasing or inventory decisions in the target SME setting. Determine the final sample size from a power analysis using pilot estimates of paired task-accuracy differences. Do not treat the 1,800 scenario rows as 1,800 people. Record role and relevant experience without collecting unnecessary personal data.

## Tasks

For each scenario, ask the participant to:
1. Decide whether to reorder now or take no action.
2. Select or confirm the order quantity where relevant.
3. Identify the main evidence behind the decision.
4. Rate confidence on a five-point scale.

Include cases with pending purchases and insufficient budget so that the explanation must distinguish on-hand stock, inventory position and feasible pack quantity.

## Measures

- **Primary:** decision correctness against the audited deterministic rule (action and quantity).
- **Secondary:** task completion time, confidence, evidence comprehension, perceived clarity and usability (for example, SUS or UMUX-Lite).
- **Qualitative:** short follow-up questions on misunderstood terms, missing evidence and trust in the recommendation.

## Analysis plan

Compare paired task accuracy with a paired binary method such as McNemar's test and report the absolute difference with a 95% confidence interval. Compare completion time and confidence with paired methods or mixed-effects models that account for repeated tasks per participant. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not p-values alone. Pre-specify exclusions and missing-response handling before recruitment.

## Ethics and data handling

Obtain supervisor and institutional ethics approval where required, provide an information sheet and informed consent, allow withdrawal, and use participant codes. Do not show customer names, phone numbers or identifiable transactions. Store recordings only with separate consent and delete them according to the approved retention plan. A human reviewer remains responsible for every purchase decision; the system must not place orders automatically.

## Readiness criteria

Before recruitment, confirm that the task set covers all decision constraints, the Bangla wording is understandable to target users, the order-quantity arithmetic is correct, and the baseline and explanation conditions contain the same underlying evidence. The present dataset audit passes the rule-consistency check, but the participant study has not been run.
