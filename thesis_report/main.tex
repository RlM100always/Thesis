\addtotoc{Abstract}
\begin{center}
  {\huge\textit{Abstract}\par}
\end{center}
This thesis develops \system{}, an AI-powered business analytics and
decision-support framework for performance evaluation, demand prediction,
customer insight and feasible recommendations in Bangladeshi SMEs. The
supplied dataset manifest identifies the business package as real SME sales
and real people. The RQ1 daily panel contains 561,152 observations across
32 shops, 16 categories and 512 shop--category series from 2023 to 2025,
with 1,580,361 observed units and 37.60\% zero-sales observations. The
forecasting target is observed sales, not unobserved latent demand.

A leakage-controlled LightGBM Tweedie experiment compared historical
series features with additional shop, category and network context features.
Across nine monthly seven-day development origins, pooled WAPE decreased
from 33.47\% to 32.66\%; the context model won six of nine origins. Its
aggregate bias increased in magnitude from -2.02\% to -6.05\%, illustrating
the trade-off between absolute error and under-forecasting. The context
model also improved pooled WAPE in the three-origin 1-, 14- and 28-day
development comparisons. Prequential interval coverage and bottom-up
hierarchical aggregation were evaluated as additional diagnostics.
These results are development evidence, not a final untouched-test claim.
SKU-level latent demand, inventory-policy benefit and customer-action
effectiveness require separate evidence and evaluation.

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter_introduction}

\section{Context and Motivation}
SMEs generate valuable evidence through sales, purchases, payments, inventory
movements and customer transactions, but these records are often distributed
across ledgers, spreadsheets and separate applications. Owners may see that
sales have changed without knowing which products, customers or operating
conditions explain the change, or what response is feasible.

The challenge extends beyond displaying historical KPIs. Retail decisions
require demand estimates while accounting for cash, stock, supplier lead time,
pack size, storage and expiry. Customer inactivity, incomplete cost records
and promotion effects create further analytical risks. This research connects
operational events to valid predictions and explainable, constraint-aware
decisions \cite{chen2012business,fildes2022retail,makridakis2022m5}.

\section{Bangladesh SME Business Context}
The intended setting is Bangladeshi SMEs in retail, wholesale, distribution
and related sectors. A shared operational core covers sales, purchases,
expenses, payments, customers and performance indicators; inventory, batch,
expiry and capacity modules apply where relevant. A common framework does not
imply that one model suits every business.

Bangladesh studies identify management support, perceived benefit, cost,
complexity and organisational readiness as important influences on SME
technology adoption \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital}. These findings
motivate low-friction data entry, local payment compatibility and owner-readable
evidence. Results remain bounded to the businesses and periods represented
in the supplied data.

\section{Decision Environment and Stakeholder Requirements}
The owner or manager makes the principal decisions, while operational,
finance and supply staff create or validate supporting records. The system
must preserve traceability from each result to its source events,
transformation rules and historical cutoff. It must distinguish incomplete
evidence from a valid zero and allow owners to inspect, modify or reject
recommendations.

The design is assessed on decision relevance, traceability, proportionality
and contestability.

\section{Problem Statement}
The central problem is the weak connection between SME operational records,
valid analytical estimates and feasible business actions. Four gaps follow:
\begin{enumerate}[label=G\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Data foundation:} records need consistent identifiers,
timestamps, provenance and business boundaries.
\item \textbf{Prediction validity:} features, labels and transformations must
respect decision-time availability and chronological evaluation
\cite{kaufman2012leakage}.
\item \textbf{Prediction to action:} forecasts and risk scores must be linked
to cost, stock, cash, lead-time and consent constraints
\cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}.
\item \textbf{Adoption and accountability:} owners need understandable,
traceable evidence and authority to reject or modify recommendations.
Feature attribution supports inspection but does not establish causality
\cite{lundberg2017shap,miller2019explanation}.
\end{enumerate}
The thesis evaluates an integrated SME decision-support framework. It does
not assume that machine learning must outperform every baseline; a simpler
method is valid when more reliable or economical for the task.

\section{Analytical Logic and Claim Boundaries}
The process separates an observed business event, an estimate based on
information available at that time, an eligible action and a later outcome.
Missing records weaken the estimate; leakage invalidates it; ignored
constraints make an action infeasible; and absent follow-up prevents a
credible impact claim.

The thesis distinguishes descriptive KPIs, predictive estimates,
prescriptive recommendations and realized business impact. Recorded sales
may be censored demand when stock is unavailable. Future inactivity means
no observed purchase within a defined window, not permanent customer loss.
Model explanations describe model behaviour, not causal effects
\cite{lundberg2017shap,miller2019explanation}. Claims are limited to the
businesses, periods and tasks actually evaluated.

\section{Research Aim and Objectives}

\subsection{General Objective}

The overall goal is to create and test an AI-based business solution. The analytics framework is designed as a solution that allows Bangladeshi SME businesses to track their business performance, extract reliable and explainable insights, and prioritise feasible strategies while maintaining owner control.


\subsection{Specific Objectives}

The study will:

\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*.]

\item specify an auditable canonical data model for sales, with a focus on the business and its activities, including products, stock, batches, expiry, purchases, returns, customers, suppliers, payments, and expenses;

\item build reproducible preprocessing and feature pipelines for performance analysis, SKU demand, customer inactivity, and segmentation;

\item compare transparent baselines, a pooled multi-business model, and shop-adapted models using chronological and held-out-business validation, with both predictive and decision-oriented measures;

\item measure uncertainty and provide explanations at both global and case levels, while recognising that association does not imply causation;

\item formulate a mechanism that retains constraint awareness in reorder, retention, and resource-allocation decisions; and

\item assess technical correctness, robustness, scalability, usability, and potential business value at distinct levels of evidence, using data collected with consent from the Bangladeshi SME sector.

\end{enumerate}


\section{Initial Solution Concept}
The proposed solution is \system{}, a multi-sector SME decision-support
framework with a shared operational core and configurable analytical tasks.
It validates business events, creates time-frozen analytical views, compares
baselines with eligible models, and presents estimates with their cutoff,
uncertainty and supporting evidence.

Its three functions follow the approved project title:
\emph{performance evaluation} reconciles sales, stock and financial indicators;
\emph{insight extraction} identifies demand patterns, customer inactivity and
operational anomalies; and \emph{strategy optimisation} ranks feasible
purchasing or customer actions under business constraints. The owner retains
final authority, and outcomes are recorded for audit and monitoring.

\section{Research Questions}

The thesis is based on four research questions:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2em]

\item[RQ1.] Do features such as calendar effects, price, promotion, and market context enhance out-of-time branch--SKU demand forecasts over seasonal and statistical baselines in the target business setting?

\item[RQ2.] How accurately and reliably can a historical customer snapshot predict whether there will be no repeat purchase within a predetermined future time period?

\item[RQ3.] Under the same demand and operating constraints, can a forecast-driven reorder policy reduce stock-outs and holding costs compared with the current policy of the participating business?

\item[RQ4.] Do short Bangla action descriptions enhance owner task-friendliness, confidence, and perceived usability without causing information overload or increasing decision time?

\end{description}


\section{Scope and Boundaries}
Empirical claims are restricted to the supplied Bangladesh business dataset
and tasks for which its fields are suitable. The current forecasting
experiment is at shop--category--day level and predicts observed sales; it
does not establish SKU-level latent demand. Customer-risk analysis requires
stable pseudonymous identifiers and complete follow-up. Inventory-policy
claims require reconciled stock, cost and supplier constraints. Cross-sector
generalisation and realized business impact require separate evaluation.

\section{Expected Contributions and Significance}

The evaluated integration of established research methods, constrained within the context and relevance of small SME businesses, is expected to be a research contribution. Specifically, the work is expected to include (i) a traceable business operational-to-analytical data design; (ii) reusable historical-snapshot protocols designed to avoid common forms of data leakage; (iii) a Bangladesh-aware comparison of forecasting and customer-inactivity approaches; and (iv) a clear link between prediction and constrained, actionable recommendations.

The work also provides evidence of the ability of owners to interpret and apply the information presented to them. When making a recommendation, the system presents supporting evidence to help the owner understand the ``how and why'' of the recommendation, while using familiar rules and maintaining the owner's control over the action. It is not intended to exclude the owner from the decision or to label a numerical output as profitable without considering the business context. Before measuring the impact on the business, the system must first establish that its outputs are accurate, interpretable, and actionable.

\section{Background Study}

\subsection{Business Intelligence and the Decision Continuum}

Business intelligence (BI) is a blend of data, infrastructure, analytical techniques, best practices, experience, and organisational practice used to enhance decision-making. Chen, Chiang, and Storey trace its evolution from enterprise reporting to web, mobile, and sensor-rich analytics \cite{chen2012business}. This thesis follows a three-step approach: \emph{descriptive analytics} determines what happened and where; \emph{predictive analytics} predicts what is likely to happen next; and \emph{prescriptive analytics} compares feasible responses.

The stages are interconnected. An action recommendation is only as believable as the prediction that supports it, and a prediction is only as believable as the operational data from which it was obtained. There are also different integrity requirements for operational and analytical workloads. While a sale requires consistent updates to stock and payment records, an analytical model requires a historical feature view containing values that would not change once the evaluation point has been reached.

The proposed framework therefore separates the transaction record from versioned analytical snapshots. It maintains the connection between them through source identifiers, timestamps, and transformation metadata.


\subsection{SME Digitalization in Bangladesh}

The decision to adopt technology is not only a matter of the accuracy of technology models. A mixed-method study indicates that the important factors determining ICT adoption among SMEs in rural Bangladesh are awareness of the benefits, government support, top-management support, and financial support \cite{hoque2016ict}. A later survey of 535 SME managers combines the technology--organization--environment model with diffusion-of-innovation concepts. The authors \cite{shahadat2023digital} recognize that factors such as relative advantage, complexity, perceived cost, management support, competitive pressure, and government support are important considerations. These studies suggest that organizational readiness and costs, as well as technological fit and workflow functionality, warrant attention when considering technology adoption. However, the two studies do not evaluate a retail prediction system.

This operating context also includes mobile financial services. Through in-depth interviews, accessibility, spatial and temporal mobility, and access to information were found to be important issues in the daily use of MFS in Bangladesh. Self-control and the affordances related to self-control are also important considerations \cite{hazra2021mfs}. Therefore, the proposed data model includes the payment mode in the settlement status field, along with other common business fields. The study should not be construed as providing evidence of better business performance for a named provider or of better performance of one local provider compared with another.



\subsection{Shared SME Core and Sector-Specific Requirements}

The research scope covers SME businesses across sectors. Shared entities
support comparable performance indicators, while sector-specific extensions
capture differences in products, service capacity, sales cycles, procurement
and operating constraints. Inventory-based firms may require SKU, stock, pack,
batch and shelf-life fields; service firms may require service type, bookings,
staff availability and delivery capacity. Fields that do not apply are marked
not applicable rather than populated with invented values.

The artifact supports business performance evaluation, insight extraction
and feasible strategy recommendations. Owners retain authority over actions.
Sector-specific requirements and owner constraints are assessed before an
analytical task is enabled; unsupported tasks return an explicit evidence gap.


\subsection{Performance Evaluation and Data Foundations}

Well-defined measures are the starting point for performance evaluation. Revenue, gross margin, average order value, stock turnover, return rate, and customer repeat rate each need to include a defined time window, an aggregation method, and appropriate treatment of events such as rescheduling, refunds, and lost fees. If any of these elements are not incorporated into a dashboard value, the resulting measure may be misleading or ignored. Definitions may also differ from the accounting records or may refer to different time periods. The canonical layer therefore maintains invoice lines and stock movements while creating analytical measures through documented views.

Data provenance concerns where a record came from, when it was received, and how it was transformed. Primary business data should be accompanied by provenance information, together with appropriate customer anonymisation and organisational anonymisation. For public data, the context and reasonable implications of the applicable licence should also be documented.


\subsection{Data Quality, Provenance and Temporal Semantics}
Analytical validity depends on the meaning and timing of each field. Event
time, recording time, correction time and decision cutoff are not
interchangeable. Historical features must use only information available by
the cutoff; later corrections or outcomes must not leak into earlier
snapshots \cite{kaufman2012leakage}. Missing records are not automatically
zero, and unknown stock exposure remains unknown.

A reproducible manifest records source, owner, collection period, schema,
checksum, row counts, exclusions and transformation rules. Reconciliation
checks keys, duplicates, quantities and links among orders, sales lines,
payments and inventory movements. These checks establish internal
consistency; source documentation establishes declared origin.

\subsection{Customer Analytics: RFM, CLV and Segmentation}

A simple summary of \emph{recency}, \emph{frequency}, and \emph{monetary value} is provided by RFM. It summarises a customer's purchase or transaction history using features that are easy to calculate and maintain an unambiguous behavioural interpretation. Fader, Hardie, and Lee link RFM with Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) through the Pareto/NBD and gamma--gamma components, demonstrating how the value of a particular customer history can be estimated \cite{fader2005rfm}. CLV can be used as an outcome or planning measure, but it is not necessarily suitable as a direct prediction target or operational goal.

A field that defines a segment should not be admitted as an independent predictor of that same segment, as this would introduce information leakage. The question for unsupervised segmentation is whether coherent behavioural groups can be identified without an existing label. K-Means aims to:


\begin{equation}
  \underset{\{C_k,\mu_k\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\operatorname{minimize}}
  \sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{x_i\in C_k}\lVert x_i-\mu_k\rVert_2^2.
  \label{eq:kmeans}
\end{equation}

The procedure returns clusters even when the data form a continuum. Therefore, both the number and the interpretation of the clusters need to be validated. The silhouette coefficient measures the similarity of observations within a cluster and their separation from the closest other cluster \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. The stability of the clustering parameters across resampling and the business usefulness of the resulting clusters should be treated as separate tests. An attractive label such as ``loyal'' does not, by itself, imply statistical stability or actionability of a cluster.


\subsection{Retail Demand Forecasting}

A demand forecast estimates the number of units needed for a specific product, location, and future horizon. Retail demand series can be influenced by price, promotion, stock-outs, festivals, assortment changes, and aggregation across products or branches. Fildes, Ma, and Kolassa emphasize that retail forecasting practice cannot be simplified into a single algorithm because of data hierarchy, operational requirements, and the amount of data involved. The forecasting process and the way forecasts are used also matter, alongside statistical accuracy \cite{fildes2022retail}.

Intermittent SKU demand contains numerous zero-demand periods between non-zero observations. Croston's method smooths the non-zero demand size and the interval between demands separately. Syntetos and Boylan examine the accuracy and bias of intermittent-demand estimates \cite{syntetos2005intermittent}. This work supports the use of an intermittent-demand baseline rather than requiring a uniform and constant baseline. For dense time series, an appropriate dense-series model can be used at the SKU level. Forecasts also need to be reconciled across different levels of aggregation, including product, category, branch, and overall business totals. Optimal-combination reconciliation provides a principled means of generating coherent hierarchical forecasts \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical}.

When modelling, the model should be only as complex as the available evidence supports. ARIMA-family models represent serial dependence, including dependence after appropriate transformations; tree ensembles can learn nonlinear effects from lagged, calendar, and price variables; and sequence models can use ordered historical observations. Random Forest and XGBoost provide suitable tabular alternatives \cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}. The M5 retail competition illustrates the importance of large-scale, hierarchical, and uncertainty-aware comparison \cite{makridakis2022m5}. A seasonal-naive forecast is therefore a necessary benchmark rather than a ceremonial baseline.

Although inventory usefulness is related to forecast accuracy, the two are not the same. Hyndman and Koehler discuss the limitations of common percentage-error measures and suggest alternative approaches, including MASE as a scale-free measure for comparison \cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. A study by Syntetos and colleagues associates the choice of forecasting methods with stock-control requirements, whereas forecasting selection based solely on statistical accuracy may not reflect the needs of inventory management \cite{syntetos2010stock}. The methods are therefore evaluated using point-forecast error, uncertainty, and simulated stock-out and holding costs rather than selecting a technique based on a single easily computed metric.


\subsection{Forecastability, Demand Regimes and Decision Loss}
Retail series differ in volume, variability, intermittency, trend and
seasonality. ADI and squared coefficient of variation describe regimes,
but labels must use only history available at the forecast origin and
should diagnose rather than automatically route models. Croston-family
methods and TSB are relevant intermittent-demand comparators
\cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent,teunter2011demand}.

Forecast accuracy and inventory performance are distinct. Lower WAPE may
not reduce shortages or holding cost when bias, lead time, pack multiples,
cash and stock constraints are ignored. Models should be compared on common
origins and targets, then evaluated through a separate policy replay
\cite{syntetos2010stock}.

\subsection{Customer Inactivity, Class Imbalance and Calibration}

Churn can be a documented cancellation for a subscription-based service. For non-contractual retail, however, churn has to be specified at a cutoff time $t$ as no repeat purchase within a given time interval $[t,t+H]$. All features must be known at time $t$, and the training and validation processes must not be contaminated by information from the future label window. This definition translates a business term into a repeatable prediction task.

In business-to-business settings, the positive class can be scarce or vary across datasets. Saito and Rehmsmeier provide a rationale for the superiority of precision--recall analysis over ROC analysis for imbalanced binary classification tasks \cite{saito2015pr}. Model comparison should therefore report class prevalence and the quality of the predictions before considering action costs and benefits.

The prescriptive layer includes an additional requirement. When using a model output in a decision calculation, ranking alone is not sufficient; the output needs to be converted into an interpretable probability or score. A probability of $0.7$ should, under appropriate calibration, correspond approximately to a 70\% frequency of the event among comparable cases. Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana examine the calibration of probability estimates from supervised learners and post-hoc calibration methods \cite{niculescu2005calibration}. The proposed framework therefore reports Brier score in addition to PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, and top-fraction lift, while also making calibration information available.




\subsection{From Customer Score to Responsible Retention Decision}

A customer-risk model does not grant permission for an action; instead, it provides a ranking or probability. For a given contact capacity, ranking quality determines whether the selected fraction contains more future inactive customers than an untargeted policy. When an expected value is calculated, the probability needs to be calibrated because the benefits and costs are subject to uncertainty and are weighted by the estimated probability. Therefore, the operational threshold should be determined using validation data and the pre-declared assumptions. The final test set should not be used to determine contact capacity or utility assumptions; rather, it should be used to confirm the choices made during the development process.

Additional ambiguity is created by the customer record. The frequency and amount of purchases may be linked to opportunities to visit, household purchasing patterns, or shared identifiers. Neither return behaviour nor discount-seeking behaviour alone is sufficient to indicate loyalty or future value. Fader, Hardie, and Lee demonstrate why combined transaction history can provide more information than a single RFM label \cite{fader2005rfm}. Segmentation can support interpretation, but a persuasive ``segment name'' is only a description of a behavioural pattern. It does not establish that the pattern is statistically stable. Therefore, clustering should be accompanied by stability checks and owner review before the resulting segment output is used for decision-making \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}.

An independent feasibility gate is also required for responsible use. A score indicating a high probability of inactivity does not, by itself, justify customer contact if consent, contact policy, or an appropriate offer is absent. The score should also never be interpreted as a causal attribution, such as ``long recency increased risk,'' or restated as a causal claim that a particular message will retain the customer. The thesis therefore treats the prediction of inactivity, the reliability of the estimated probability, contact eligibility, and subsequent customer response as separate links in the decision process. This structure allows the model to be scientifically informative even when the available evidence is insufficient to support a claim about profit or the causal effect of a retention action.


\subsection{Leakage, Explainability and Change Over Time}

Data leakage occurs when training is exposed to information that would not legitimately exist at prediction time. It can enter through target-derived features, customers who appear on both sides of a data split, post-customer-action information, post-outcome variables, or transformations that are fitted before the data are split \cite{kaufman2012leakage}. Leakage control is therefore a characteristic of the data pipeline, not a model option. Before training, the system should define the prediction time, feature-availability rules, and data-splitting policy.

SHAP provides additive feature contributions relative to a reference value and supports both local and global inspection of model predictions \cite{lundberg2017shap}. Such an attribution describes how the fitted model uses encoded features; it does not establish a causal relationship. In social-science research, as synthesized by Miller, the focus is often on useful explanations that are selective and contrastive \cite{miller2019explanation}. Accordingly, a recommendation card should respond to the question, ``Why this action now?'' by presenting a small number of salient, decision-relevant factors. It should avoid causal language while providing sufficient supporting evidence for the owner to assess the recommendation.

Business conditions also evolve over time. Retail monitoring therefore needs to distinguish between data-quality failure, input-distribution shift, and deterioration in outcome performance. A model should not be considered valid simply because it remains on a retraining schedule. Evidence that a model remains valid should come from continued evaluation under a declared validation protocol. Any retrained model should be evaluated using the same validation principles that were established during development, with changes explicitly documented.

Once a prediction is made, the next step is to take an action subject to business constraints. Prescriptive analytics incorporates predictions into an explicit decision problem. Bertsimas and Kallus develop prescriptions based on data with auxiliary information, where observations are used to inform conditional stochastic optimization \cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}. The present thesis follows the underlying principle of evaluating actions according to expected benefit, cost, and constraints. It does not claim to reproduce the asymptotic method or theoretical approach of the cited work. Instead, reorder and retention decisions are treated as separate action families because their outcomes, costs, permissions, and uncertainties differ.

The proposed system is both an information-system artifact and an analytical study. Design-science research develops and evaluates artifacts in relation to a relevant problem and an existing knowledge base \cite{hevner2004design}. This perspective contributes to an evaluation plan that considers predictive validity, software behaviour, usability, and the consequences of decisions made by the owner, rather than treating a single model metric as the definitive criterion of success.

The literature supports five connected requirements: reliable operational
data, time-valid prediction, appropriate baselines, constraint-aware action
and accountable owner use. Bangladesh adoption studies inform local design;
forecasting and customer-analytics studies inform model evaluation; leakage,
calibration and explanation research inform validity; and prescriptive
analytics links estimates to decisions. The contribution is their integration
and evaluation within the available SME evidence, not a newly invented
algorithm.

\section{Multi-Sector Applicability and Validation}

The intended scope is SME business analytics across sectors, rather than one
industry. A common core models organizations, branches, transactions, expenses,
payments, customers and suppliers. Configurable sector extensions preserve
differences in products, services, inventory, resource capacity and operating
rules. Demand and reorder experiments apply to inventory-based firms; service
firms require service-demand and capacity tasks. Customer-risk tasks require
stable pseudonymous identifiers and adequate follow-up in either setting.

Sampling and reporting will retain sector and business identifiers. Results
will be reported per business and per sector before aggregation. Pooled models
will be compared with sector-specific and business-adapted alternatives using
the same chronological cutoffs. Held-out-business tests assess transfer within
represented sectors; held-out-sector tests assess transfer across sectors when
enough independent sectors and businesses are available. In their absence,
cross-sector transfer remains untested. Preprocessing is fitted only on training
data, and firms with many rows must not dominate business-level conclusions.
No universal accuracy or business-value claim follows from framework coverage.

\section{Research Approach}
\label{sec:research_approach}

\subsection{Research Strategy and Design-Science Rationale}

This thesis adopts a design-science research approach supported by empirical
evaluation. Design science is appropriate because the study develops an artifact
intended to address an applied organizational problem and evaluates that artifact
against explicitly defined requirements \cite{hevner2004design}. The principal
artifact is the \system{} framework, including its canonical operational data
design, time-aware analytical pipeline, constraint-aware recommendation process,
and mechanisms for presenting supporting evidence to the owner.

Empirical evaluation is necessary because a plausible technical architecture
alone is insufficient to establish predictive validity, business usefulness,
operational feasibility, or usability. Accordingly, the research evaluates not
only whether the proposed system can be implemented, but also whether its
analytical outputs remain temporally valid, whether its recommendations respect
the operating constraints of participating businesses, and whether intended
users can interpret and appropriately use those recommendations.


\subsection{Research Stages and Units of Analysis}
The work follows four stages: define the problem and evidence; design the
operational schema, snapshots, models and decision rules; evaluate data
integrity, prediction and robustness; and assess decision consequences and
owner use. Figure~\ref{fig:research_lifecycle} summarises the sequence.
Later findings may refine the artifact but cannot alter a frozen final test.

The unit of analysis depends on the task: a shop--category or SKU time series
for forecasting, a pseudonymous customer snapshot for inactivity, and an
eligible action under declared business constraints for strategy evaluation.
Evidence from one unit is not treated as proof at another level.

\subsection{Design Requirements and Acceptance Logic}
The artifact is assessed against eight requirements:
\begin{enumerate}[label=DR\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Decision relevance:} identify user, unit, horizon and decision.
\item \textbf{Operational integrity:} preserve identifiers, event times and corrections.
\item \textbf{Temporal validity:} use only information available at the cutoff.
\item \textbf{Proportionate modelling:} compare every candidate with a baseline.
\item \textbf{Uncertainty:} report intervals, calibration and sensitivity where relevant.
\item \textbf{Feasible action:} enforce stock, cash, lead-time, pack, expiry and consent constraints.
\item \textbf{Human authority:} allow owners to inspect, modify or reject actions.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility:} retain data, feature, model and output versions.
\end{enumerate}
Acceptance is non-compensatory: accuracy cannot compensate for leakage,
privacy failure or infeasible action. Requirements are classified as met,
partially met, not met or not evaluable. Missing evidence reduces the
strength of a conclusion rather than being replaced by an assumption.

\subsection{Research Questions, Hypotheses, and Measures}

The research questions introduced in Chapter~\ref{chapter_introduction} are
operationalized as follows:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2em]

    \item[RQ1 / H1]
    A business demand model incorporating locally available calendar, price,
    promotion, and market-context features will reduce out-of-time WAPE or MASE
    relative to both an otherwise identical model without those feature groups
    and a seasonal-naive benchmark.

    \item[RQ2 / H2]
    A model trained from historically valid customer snapshots will predict
    future inactivity better than an event base-rate or rule-based baseline, as
    measured by PR-AUC, and will achieve Lift@10\% greater than 1.0 without
    unacceptable deterioration in probability calibration.

    \item[RQ3 / H3]
    Under identical demand paths, costs, and operational constraints, the
    proposed forecast-based replenishment policy will reduce the combined
    consequences of stock-outs and inventory holding relative to the current
    replenishment policy used by the participating owner.

    \item[RQ4 / H4]
    In counterbalanced owner decision tasks, an explanation-supported condition
    will improve decision accuracy or confidence without causing a practically
    detrimental increase in task-completion time relative to an output-only
    condition.
\end{description}

\subsection{Target Population and Sampling Plan}

The target population consists of SMEs operating in Bangladesh across
retail, wholesale, distribution and service sectors. Recruitment will seek
multiple independent businesses and record sector, size and operating model.
An initial three-to-five-business pilot can test collection and usability,
but cannot establish population-wide or cross-sector effectiveness. Broader
sector comparisons require adequate independent businesses in each stratum.

The intended analytical target is approximately 12--24 months of longitudinal
records across participating businesses, subject to data availability, data
quality, owner consent, and ethics approval. A business with substantially less
history may still contribute to testing data collection, mapping, schema, and
data-quality procedures, but such a business shall not independently determine
claims of temporal or cross-business generalization.

Businesses will be purposively recruited because participation depends on access
to relevant longitudinal evidence, including invoice, stock, purchase, batch,
and expiry information where available, together with owner agreement. At a
minimum, included transaction data shall contain interpretable dates, product
identifiers, quantities, and sufficient contextual information to reconstruct
the relevant business process. Participating businesses shall also have an owner
or responsible operator capable of describing the current replenishment process.

Customer-level analysis imposes additional eligibility requirements. It requires
stable pseudonymous customer identifiers and sufficient follow-up after each
historical cutoff to determine the relevant outcome. If a business, analytical
task, or time period does not satisfy the minimum evidence requirements for a
specific analysis, that unit will be excluded from that analysis rather than
being repaired through invented identifiers, fabricated constraints, or assumed
outcomes.

Each incoming dataset will be accompanied by a manifest recording its source,
owner or repository, collection period, receipt date, checksum, licence or
agreement, row count, schema version, mapping rules, exclusions, and known
limitations.

Tier-3 generated data will never be mixed into a Tier-1 test set. If generated
or augmented records are used during model development, results from real-only
training and augmented training will be reported separately and evaluated
against the same untouched real test period wherever the required real evidence
is available.


\subsection{Data Acquisition and Variable Availability}
The supplied package contains operational sales, customer, product, purchase,
payment, inventory, return, promotion and calendar records. RQ1 uses the
complete daily shop--category observed-sales panel. Other tables are joined
only after their keys, event times and business meaning are checked.

Each variable is classified by availability time. Price, promotion or supplier
conditions are predictors only when known at the forecast origin. Later sales,
returns and inventory movements are outcomes, not predictors. Raw business
files remain in controlled storage; the repository contains code, schemas,
checksums and aggregate results.

\subsection{Data-Quality Assessment and Task Eligibility}
The ingestion audit validates required fields, types, identifiers, duplicate
keys, date ranges, non-negative quantities and cross-table relationships.
Sales lines are reconciled to parent orders; inventory sale movements are
checked against sales lines; and daily aggregates are compared with
transaction-level reconstruction. Mismatches are reported and quarantined,
not silently repaired.

For the complete shop--category panel, the audit checks unique
shop--category--date keys, required values, consistency of the zero-sales
flag and a complete daily calendar. This panel is eligible for observed-sales
forecasting at its declared aggregation level. It does not automatically
make sparse SKU exports eligible for zero-demand labels.

SKU exposure requires evidence that the product was active, the shop was
operating and stock was available. Missing SKU dates are not automatically
zero. Negative reconstructed balances, incomplete inventory coverage,
unknown assortment, closed periods and stock-out periods remain censored
or unknown until reconciled. RQ1 observed-sales forecasting can proceed on
the complete panel; SKU latent-demand estimation, customer inactivity and
inventory-policy replay each require their own validated inputs.

\subsection{Ethics, Privacy, and Governance}

University ethics approval and an appropriate signed data-sharing agreement will
be obtained before primary data collection begins. Direct identifiers such as
customer names, phone numbers, email addresses, national identifiers, account
numbers, or mobile-wallet numbers are not required for the research analytical
tables.

Where customer-level analysis is permitted, each participating business will use
a separate salted pseudonymous customer key. Any mapping capable of
re-identification will remain under the control of the participating business
and will not be stored in the research repository.

The data-sharing agreement will specify the research purpose, authorized
researchers, storage arrangements, retention period, publication format,
withdrawal conditions, and deletion procedures. Access to research data will
follow least-privilege principles, while published outputs will consist of
aggregated results or individually reviewed de-identified examples.

A model-generated customer-retention recommendation does not itself authorize
contact with that customer. Customer consent and the participating business's
contact policy remain independent feasibility constraints. Similarly, system
outputs are intended to support rather than replace the decision-making authority
of the owner.


\subsection{Canonical Operational and Analytical Model}
The operational model links businesses and shops to products, customers,
suppliers, sales orders, sales lines, payments, purchase orders, inventory
movements, returns and promotions. Stable identifiers preserve relationships.
Analytical tables are derived at a declared grain and cutoff; they are not
independent source-of-truth records.

The implemented RQ1 grain is shop--category--day. Its target is observed
units sold. SKU-level demand requires separate assortment and stock-exposure
validation before missing dates can be interpreted.

\section{Formal Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formal_problem_formulation}

Let \(b\) denote a business, \(l\) a branch, \(p\) a product, \(r\) an
inventory batch, \(c\) a customer, and \(t\) a decision time. Let

\begin{equation}
D_t=
\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Sales, Stock, Batches, Expiry, Purchases, Expenses,}\\
\text{Customers, Constraints, and Context}
\end{array}
\right\}_{\leq t},
\label{eq:data_cutoff}
\end{equation}

denote the governed evidence available at or before decision time \(t\).
Accordingly, any analytical output produced at \(t\) must be derived only from
information contained in \(D_t\).


\subsection{Performance Evaluation}
\label{subsec:performance_evaluation}

For each KPI \(K\), the system returns \(K(D_t;W,g)\), where \(W\) is the
reporting window and \(g\) is the aggregation level. Each KPI retains its
numerator, denominator, filters, definition version, and data-quality status so
that the owner can reproduce and inspect the calculation.

For reporting window \(W\), net sales are calculated from valid invoice lines
using the documented business treatment of discounts, cancellations, and
returns. A general form is

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}
=
\sum_{i\in W} q_i p_i
-
\sum_{i\in W} d_i
-
\sum_{r\in W} v_r,
\label{eq:netsales}
\end{equation}

where \(q_i p_i\) is the gross value of invoice line \(i\), \(d_i\) is the
recognized discount, and \(v_r\) is the value of a valid return or reversal
attributed to the reporting window.

Gross margin is reported only when sufficiently reliable cost evidence is
available:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GrossMarginRate}_{W}
=
\frac{
\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}-\mathrm{COGS}_{W}
}{
\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}
},
\label{eq:grossmargin}
\end{equation}

provided that \(\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}\neq 0\).

If cost information is unavailable for a material share of sold units, the
system reports sales and cost coverage rather than silently interpreting revenue
as profit. Average order value uses eligible completed orders as its
denominator; repeat-rate reporting states customer-identification coverage; and
stock-turnover reporting specifies both the cost and average-inventory
conventions used. Period comparisons use comparable windows or explicitly
identify differences in trading days and closures.

Each KPI response therefore contains a value, definition version, reporting
period, aggregation level, coverage indicators, and reconciliation status.
This structure makes changes in business-rule definitions visible and allows
the evaluation to distinguish calculation anomalies from genuine changes in
business performance.


\subsection{Demand Prediction}
\label{subsec:demand_prediction}
The implemented RQ1 target is observed units at shop--category--day level.
For series \(s\), origin \(t\) and horizon \(h\in\{1,7,14,28\}\), the direct
cumulative target is
\[
Y_{s,t,h}=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}y_{s,t+j}.
\]
Separate models are fitted for each horizon. Features must be available
strictly before \(t\), and every training target window must end before
its validation origin.

Historical features include lags 1, 7, 14, 28 and 56; rolling mean,
standard deviation, sum and zero ratio over 7, 14, 28 and 56 days; days
since last positive sale; ADI; positive-demand mean and \(CV^2\); shop and
category identifiers; and calendar encodings. The context-aware challenger
adds past shop, category and network aggregates, recent trends and the
series' share of those aggregates. Context features use only observations
before the origin.

Candidates include seasonal-naive, Croston--SBA, TSB, LightGBM Tweedie,
CatBoost Tweedie and a two-stage occurrence--quantity model. ADI and \(CV^2\)
support diagnosis but do not automatically route a model. Specialist methods
are promoted only with adequate common-origin validation and independent
series support.

The forecast estimates observed sales, not unconstrained latent demand.
Stock-out censoring, promotion effects and SKU-level forecasts require
additional point-in-time evidence and separate validation.

\subsection{Adaptive Forecasting Architecture and Promotion Gates}
The architecture separates source eligibility, target construction,
candidate forecasts, model selection and business action. Source and target
gates distinguish observed zero from censored or unknown demand. Candidates
are compared on common rolling origins, bias and independent-series support.
Uncertainty and aggregation follow selection; inventory actions require
validated costs, lead time, stock and owner constraints.

\subsection{Future Customer Inactivity}
\label{subsec:customer_inactivity}

For customer \(c\) at historical cutoff \(t\), future inactivity is represented
as

\begin{equation}
q_{c,t}
=
P\!\left(
\text{no purchase in }(t,t+H]
\mid
X_{c,t}
\right),
\label{eq:churn}
\end{equation}

where \(X_{c,t}\) contains only records available by cutoff \(t\), and \(H\)
is the predefined future outcome horizon. The value of \(H\) will be fixed
before final evaluation with reference to the relevant business purchase cycle.

The resulting \(q_{c,t}\) represents predicted inactivity risk and does not, by
itself, estimate the causal effect of contacting customer \(c\).


\subsection{Strategy Optimization}
\label{subsec:strategy_optimization}

For an eligible action \(a\in A_t\), the system evaluates

\begin{equation}
U(a\mid D_t)
=
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta\mathrm{Profit}(a)\right]
-
\mathrm{ActionCost}(a)
-
\lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(a),
\label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}

subject to operational constraints including budget, storage, minimum-order
quantity, supplier lead time, pack multiple, remaining shelf life, expiry,
temperature-controlled storage capacity, incoming stock, available inventory, and customer-contact
consent where applicable.

This transparent objective represents the transition from predictive outputs to
prescriptive decisions. Its terms will be estimated using available evidence,
and uncertain values will be examined through sensitivity analysis rather than
treated as precisely known constants.


\subsubsection{Inventory Position and Replenishment Cost}
Inventory position combines usable on-hand stock and confirmed incoming
quantity, less committed demand. Replenishment is constrained by supplier
lead time, pack multiple, minimum order quantity, budget, storage, expiry
and remaining shelf life. An action is withheld when a required field is
unknown or a hard constraint is violated.

Policy evaluation compares the owner policy and proposed policy on the same
demand path and opening inventory. Outcomes include stock-out units, average
inventory, holding cost, shortage cost, expiry and total cost. Forecast error
alone is not interpreted as inventory benefit.

\subsubsection{Customer Action Logic and Abstention}

Customer-action logic differs from replenishment logic. The inactivity
probability \(q_{c,t}\) in Equation~\ref{eq:churn} may be used to prioritize
customers for review, but it does not estimate the causal effect of contacting
customer \(c\).

A contact action is eligible only when customer consent and business policy
permit contact, the proposed treatment is specified, and sufficient operational
capacity exists. Expected profit will be calculated only when a defensible
estimate of incremental response benefit is available. Otherwise, the output
will explicitly remain a risk-ranked contact list. This separation prevents a
predictive probability from being incorrectly interpreted as campaign uplift.

The action layer may also abstain from making a recommendation. Abstention is
appropriate when required constraints are missing, uncertainty makes the leading
alternatives practically indistinguishable, predicted benefit does not exceed
action cost over the declared sensitivity range, or the relevant model fails a
monitoring gate. An abstention accompanied by a reason is therefore treated as
valid safety behaviour rather than as a system error.


\subsection{Preprocessing and Historical Snapshots}
\label{subsec:preprocessing_snapshots}

The processing sequence is fixed: validate the source and schema; remove or flag
exact duplicates; reconcile cancellations and returns; construct the data split;
fit imputation, encoding, and scaling transformations using training data only;
construct features; train candidate models; and evaluate them on the untouched
test set. This ordering prevents information from future periods from leaking
into model development \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

Demand features may include lagged demand, rolling location and variability
statistics, day of week, month, distance to relevant festivals, price,
discount, known promotions, stock-out indicators, pack units, remaining shelf
life, and external market context. Batch-expiry and temperature-controlled storage variables
primarily constrain action feasibility and enter prediction only when their
decision-time availability and analytical role are defensible.

Where feasible, zero observed sales caused by zero demand will be distinguished
from zero sales caused by lack of stock. Intermittency will be measured before
selecting temporal granularity and candidate forecasting methods.

Customer snapshots may include RFM variables, tenure, order-value statistics,
category breadth, purchase intervals, discount behaviour, and pre-cutoff return
rates. The outcome label is constructed from a subsequent future window. Where
required, a purge interval will separate the observation and label windows to
prevent overlap or delayed events from introducing leakage.

Customer identifiers used for an entity-generalization test will not appear on
both sides of the corresponding training--test boundary.


\section{Candidate Methods and Selection Rules}
\label{sec:candidate_methods}


\subsection{Baseline Ladder and Task-Specific Candidates}

Method selection follows a baseline ladder, with model eligibility determined by
the available evidence volume and task structure.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Performance:}
    Auditable KPI definitions and rule-based anomaly detection provide the
    descriptive baseline.

    \item \textbf{Demand:}
    Last-value, weekly seasonal-naive, moving-average, and exponential-smoothing
    baselines are evaluated before ARIMA/SARIMA, Croston--SBA, TSB, and
    gradient-boosted lag models. LightGBM and CatBoost are candidates for pooled
    shop/category or SKU features when the sample and categorical structure
    support them. LSTM models are considered only when sequence volume and
    history justify their additional complexity. Intermittent-demand methods
    are promoted only after same-origin validation
    \cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent,teunter2011demand,
    ke2017lightgbm}.

    \item \textbf{Inactivity:}
    A recency or base-rate rule is compared with Logistic Regression,
    Random Forest \cite{breiman2001rf}, and XGBoost
    \cite{chen2016xgboost}. Class weighting and decision thresholds are selected
    using development data only.

    \item \textbf{Segmentation:}
    Appropriately scaled RFM features are analysed using K-Means. Candidate
    values of \(K\) are evaluated using inertia, silhouette, resampling
    stability, and business interpretability.

    \item \textbf{Explanation:}
    Coefficient- or rule-based explanations are preferred for simple models,
    while SHAP provides model-behaviour attributions for compatible tree-based
    models \cite{lundberg2017shap}. Displayed explanations will remain selective
    and context-specific \cite{miller2019explanation}.

\end{itemize}


\subsection{Model Eligibility and Sector-Aware SME Adaptation}
Candidates are compared only when target, origin, horizon, training
information and evaluation rows are aligned. Every task begins with a
baseline. Pooled models may borrow information across businesses; business
adaptation requires sufficient independent history.

Selection considers error, aggregate bias, calibration, stability across
origins and independent-series support. Repeated forecasts of one series
do not create independent businesses. Regime labels support diagnosis but
do not justify automatic specialist routing when subgroup support is sparse.
Sector-specific extensions are enabled only when relevant fields and
operating rules are available.

\section{The \system{} Sequential Procedure}
\label{sec:sequential_procedure}


\subsection{Strategy Recommendation Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\system{} Strategy Recommendation}
\label{alg:bsmart}

\KwIn{
Data available at cutoff \(D_t\), horizon \(H\), constraints \(C_t\),
validated model/baseline versions \(M\), and candidate actions \(A\)
}

\KwOut{
Ranked feasible recommendations \(R_t\)
}

Check sources, schema, timestamps, and organizational ownership\;

Create the historical feature snapshot \(X_t\)\;

Apply transformations fitted on the appropriate training evidence\;

Compare eligible baseline, pooled-business, and shop-adapted candidates\;

Estimate demand uncertainty and customer inactivity probabilities\;

Compute reproducible KPIs and data-quality alerts\;

Generate candidate actions with source and decision-time metadata\;

\ForEach{\(a\in A\)}{

    Check budget, stock, pack, applicable shelf-life, storage, lead-time, capacity, and consent
    feasibility under \(C_t\)\;

    Estimate benefit, cost, and uncertainty using declared assumptions\;

    \(U(a)\leftarrow
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta\mathrm{Profit}(a)\right]
    -
    \mathrm{Cost}(a)
    -
    \lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(a)\)\;
}

Remove infeasible actions and rank the remaining actions by \(U(a)\)\;

Attach a concise explanation, uncertainty statement, cutoff, and evidence
source\;

Return the top-\(K\) actions and record the owner's response and any subsequent
observable outcome\;

\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Computational Complexity}

For \(N\) invoice lines, \(d\) validated fields, and \(P\) candidate actions,
source validation and feature aggregation are approximately

\begin{equation}
O(Nd),
\label{eq:validation_complexity}
\end{equation}

while ranking the candidate actions requires approximately

\begin{equation}
O(P\log P).
\label{eq:ranking_complexity}
\end{equation}

The computational complexity of model training depends on the selected model and
will be measured empirically. Serving uses versioned artifacts and therefore
does not retrain the model within the request path.

\subsection{Frozen Final Evaluation and Outcome Maturation}
\label{subsec:frozen_evaluation}

Training and validation snapshots are used for model development. The final test
set remains sealed until the task definition, feature and eligibility rules,
candidate models, primary metrics, decision thresholds, and random seeds have
been frozen.

If a defect is identified after the final test has been opened, the defect and
its impact will be documented. The affected observations cannot subsequently be
treated as though they were still part of an untouched final test. This rule
protects the interpretation of the final result rather than merely enforcing the
mechanics of a data split.

Outcome records are not automatically incorporated into a released model.
Observed outcomes first enter the monitoring store. They become eligible for a
later model version only after reconciliation, label maturation, and repetition
of the appropriate validation protocol.

This delay ensures that an owner's response, a corrected invoice, or a partially
observed outcome cannot retrospectively change the evidence that supported a
historical production decision.


\section{Owner-Facing Recommendation Evidence Contract}
\label{sec:recommendation_contract}

\subsection{Minimum Recommendation Evidence}
Each recommendation identifies its business, decision, target, cutoff,
model or rule version, uncertainty and supporting evidence. Reorder
suggestions expose current and incoming stock, forecast horizon, lead time,
pack requirements, budget and expiry risk. Customer actions expose the
historical behaviour supporting the priority, probability reliability and
consent status.

When a required field is missing, the system withholds the action or labels
the result incomplete. It does not assume costs, stock or customer responses.
Owners may accept, reject, modify or defer actions; responses and later
observable outcomes are retained for audit.

\subsection{Human Use and Accountability}
\label{subsec:human_use_accountability}

An explanation is considered successful only when it contributes to correct and
appropriate use. The owner study will therefore include scenarios in which the
appropriate decision is to reject or defer an apparently attractive
recommendation.

This evaluates calibrated human reliance rather than merely whether participants
prefer a more detailed interface. The evidence contract also supports failure
analysis because an inappropriate decision can be traced to the underlying
estimate, missing constraint, explanation, outdated evidence, or owner
interpretation rather than being attributed generically to the ``AI.''

\subsection{Forecasting Evaluation}
\label{subsec:forecasting_evaluation}

All forecasting splits preserve temporal order. Candidate methods are selected
using rolling-origin or expanding-window validation, followed by one evaluation
on a final untouched period.

For observed values \(y_i\) and forecasts \(\hat{y}_i\), the principal error
measures include

\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE}
&=
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left|y_i-\hat{y}_i\right|,
\\[4pt]
\mathrm{RMSE}
&=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left(y_i-\hat{y}_i\right)^2
},
\\[4pt]
\mathrm{WAPE}
&=
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left|y_i-\hat{y}_i\right|
}{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left|y_i\right|
},
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i|>0.
\end{align}

MASE will additionally support scale-independent comparison and avoids several
limitations of conventional percentage errors
\cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. For the current cumulative-horizon benchmark, the
reported horizon-scaled weekly MASE uses the per-series mean absolute lag-7
difference multiplied by the forecast horizon. Cluster bootstrap resampling
is performed at the shop--category-series level to preserve repeated-origin
structure. Prediction-interval coverage and interval width will be used to
evaluate uncertainty.

Results will be stratified by demand volume and intermittency so that poor
performance on low-volume or sparse products is not hidden inside an aggregate
average.


\subsection{Customer and Segmentation Evaluation}
\label{subsec:customer_segmentation_evaluation}

Customer-inactivity models will report outcome prevalence, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC,
Lift@10\%, Brier score, and probability-reliability analysis. Precision--recall
measures are emphasized under class imbalance
\cite{saito2015pr}, while calibration is evaluated independently because a model
may produce useful rankings while still producing misleading probabilities
\cite{niculescu2005calibration}.

Decision thresholds and contact fractions will be selected using validation
evidence and declared cost assumptions. They will not be modified after the
final test outcomes have been inspected.

Clustering evaluation will report inertia, silhouette, cluster stability,
minimum cluster size, and corresponding RFM profiles. Final segment
interpretations will also be reviewed with domain participants.

If separation is weak or unstable, the result will be reported as such. The
existence of algorithmically generated clusters will not be treated as evidence
that natural or causal customer types exist.


\subsection{Ablation, Robustness, and Statistical Analysis}
\label{subsec:ablation_robustness}

Planned ablations include local-calendar features, price and promotion features,
market context, pack and expiry context, and customer-history extensions.

Comparisons will include the transparent baseline, eligible pooled-business
models, and shop-adapted models where the available evidence permits them.
Where augmentation is used, real-only and augmented variants will be evaluated
under controlled comparisons.

Results will be presented by business, with paired comparisons conducted over
common forecast origins or comparable analytical instances. A held-out-business
transfer test will be used where sufficient participating businesses are
available.

Uncertainty will be summarized using paired block-bootstrap intervals across
time or business units, depending on the relevant dependence structure.
Differences between models will be reported using effect estimates and
uncertainty intervals rather than significance labels alone.

Sensitivity analysis will vary supplier lead time, shortage cost, holding cost,
action cost, and the action-risk penalty used in
Equation~\ref{eq:utility}.


\subsection{Frozen Analysis and Statistical Decision Rules}
\label{subsec:frozen_analysis_rules}
One primary comparison and metric per research question are frozen before
the final test. RQ1 uses paired out-of-time errors on common origins; RQ2
uses PR-AUC with calibration as a separate criterion; RQ3 compares policies
on identical demand paths and constraints; and RQ4 evaluates task correctness
under counterbalanced owner tasks.

Uncertainty methods must respect dependence: neighbouring dates, repeated
customer snapshots and observations from one business are not independent.
Paired block-bootstrap or cluster methods are selected according to the unit
of analysis. Effects are reported with intervals and interpreted for practical
importance, not significance labels alone.

\subsection{Practical Importance and Missing Outcomes}
\label{subsec:practical_importance}

Practical importance will be evaluated independently of statistical evidence.
A small reduction in average forecasting error may be operationally important
for a high-volume product but irrelevant after ordering constraints are applied.
Similarly, a moderate increase in task-completion time may be acceptable if it
produces a material improvement in decision correctness.

Where defensible cost or effect thresholds can be established with participating
businesses, they will be specified before the final comparison. Where no
defensible threshold exists, the magnitude and uncertainty of the effect will be
reported without assigning an arbitrary success label.

Secondary metrics, alternative horizons, customer segments, and feature
interactions remain useful for diagnosis but will be identified as secondary or
exploratory analyses. When many related comparisons are examined, the full set
of relevant results will be reported rather than only favourable cases.

Any post-test change to a model, threshold, feature definition, or analysis
creates a new exploratory version rather than replacing the original primary
result. This separation allows continued model improvement without rewriting
the original evidence.

Missing outcomes will be explicitly identified. An incomplete future horizon
cannot automatically be treated as evidence of customer inactivity. An unknown
inventory balance cannot be treated as zero stock. Similarly, a recommendation
without a later observable outcome may support a usability analysis but cannot
support a claim of realized business impact.

Where assumptions can reasonably be bounded, sensitivity analysis will be used.
Otherwise, the corresponding claim will be classified as not evaluable with the
available evidence.


\subsection{Decision, Usability, and Scalability Evaluation}
Replenishment policies are compared on identical demand paths and opening
inventory using stock-out units, average inventory, holding cost, shortage
cost, expiry and total cost. Retention value is estimated only when
incremental response, contact cost and consent evidence exist; otherwise
the task remains prioritisation, not realized-profit estimation.

Owner evaluation measures decision correctness, completion time, confidence,
error count and usability. Generated test records may support software-load
and scalability checks, but are not mixed with empirical forecast or
business-impact results.

\subsection{Monitoring Plan}
\label{subsec:monitoring_plan}

Monitoring will distinguish among schema or data-quality failure, input
distribution shift, calibration change, and outcome-performance deterioration.

An alert does not automatically authorize retraining. Any replacement model must
pass the validation procedure again and retain its training window, feature
version, parameters, and comparison results.

This requirement ensures that adaptation is evaluated before deployment rather
than assumed to be beneficial merely because new data have become available.


\section{Reproducibility, Audit, and Failure Reporting}
\label{sec:reproducibility_audit}

\subsection{Reproducibility Levels}
\label{subsec:reproducibility_levels}

Four levels of reproducibility will be designed.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Data reproducibility:}
    requires a source manifest, checksum, schema version, mapping rules, and
    exclusion log.

    \item \textbf{Analytical reproducibility:}
    requires snapshot identifiers, fitted transformations, feature definitions,
    target construction, and split manifests.

    \item \textbf{Model reproducibility:}
    requires code, execution environment, parameters, random seeds, trained
    artifacts, and the evaluation command.

    \item \textbf{Decision reproducibility:}
    additionally requires the applicable constraints, cost assumptions,
    candidate-action set, ranking rule, and interface version.

\end{itemize}


\subsection{Restricted and Shareable Evidence}
\label{subsec:restricted_shareable_evidence}

Primary partner data do not need to be publicly released in order for the study
to maintain reproducibility controls. The reproducibility package will
therefore distinguish restricted evidence from shareable artifacts.

Shareable materials may include schemas, data dictionaries, manifest templates,
transformation code, generated test fixtures, and configuration files without
revealing confidential identifiers or transaction records.

Where restricted evidence is available to an authorized evaluator, the
available package should permit that evaluator to reconstruct the relevant
snapshot and result. An external reader should additionally be able to inspect
the process using a public or generated test fixture.


\subsection{Experiment Records and Correction History}
\label{subsec:experiment_records}

Each experiment will generate a machine-readable run record containing, where
applicable, the research question, dataset and snapshot hashes, training and
test windows, grouping keys, feature version, candidate and baseline versions,
parameters, output-artifact hashes, warnings, and start and finish times.

Manual spreadsheet corrections will not be made outside this record. If an
owner supplies a correction, it becomes a dated source or mapping revision.
The correction affects downstream outputs only when a new version is executed.


\subsection{Failure Reporting}
\label{subsec:failure_reporting}

Failure reporting is part of the scientific output. Data-eligibility failure,
lack of convergence, numerical instability, training failure, resource
exhaustion, and constraint infeasibility will be distinguished from ordinary
evaluation outcomes.

The number of attempted cases will be reported. Results will not be summarized
only over successful model runs while silently excluding failures.

A method that fails on sparse or short series will therefore be evaluated
against the full eligible set to which it was applied, rather than only the
subset for which it returned a prediction.


\subsection{Recommendation Auditability and Claim Traceability}
\label{subsec:claim_traceability}

Auditability extends to the owner interface. Every recommendation receives an
identifier linking the evidence contract, displayed recommendation, owner
response, and any later observable outcome.

If a recommendation changes following a data correction or model replacement,
the previous record remains immutable and the successor record refers to it.
This supports accountability without implying that the model, researcher, or
owner alone caused the eventual business outcome.

The final research package will also include a claim-to-evidence index. Each
central claim will identify the corresponding research question, figure or
table, experimental record, and evidence tier.

Claims supported only by public, generated, simulated, or small-sample evidence
will remain explicitly labelled as such. This index provides a final safeguard
against expanding the scope of a claim beyond what was actually evaluated.


\section{Threats to Validity and Planned Controls}
The main threats are source-field errors, incomplete inventory exposure,
business heterogeneity, temporal leakage, sparse demand, uncertain costs and
limited independent businesses. Controls include transaction reconciliation,
task eligibility, point-in-time features, common-origin baselines,
cluster-aware uncertainty, sensitivity analysis and an untouched final test.

The supplied records support the current shop--category observed-sales
experiment. They do not alone establish SKU latent demand, causal promotion
effects, inventory-policy savings or transfer to every SME sector. Those
claims require additional validated fields or separate participant-based
evaluation. Unavailable evidence is reported as a limitation.

\section{Expected Methodological Outputs}
\label{sec:expected_methodological_outputs}

At completion, the methodology is expected to produce a documented canonical
business schema and data dictionary, a versioned feature and partner-data
protocol, and explicit specifications for transparent baselines,
pooled-business models, and eligible shop-adapted models.

The study will also produce a reproducible evaluation package, a
constraint-aware recommendation procedure, and an owner-study instrument.

Effectiveness claims will be restricted to the sampled businesses,
sectors, tasks and periods actually evaluated. Transfer to a new sector requires
schema and constraint mapping, new evidence and held-out-sector evaluation.

The system supports business decisions under owner oversight; automated
actions outside the evaluated business tasks remain excluded.

Implementation details, observed numerical results, and conclusions will be
reported only when the corresponding empirical evidence is available.

\chapter{Implementation}

\section{Dataset Package and Research Scope}

The supplied dataset manifest identifies the package as 100\% real SME sales
and real people, with Bangladesh as the business context, BDT currency and
coverage from 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2025. It lists 32 shops, 11,000
customers, 480 products and 56 suppliers. The source package includes
365,447 sales orders, 736,683 sales lines, 381,756 payments, 122,422 purchase
orders and purchase lines, 994,808 inventory movements, 134,379 inventory
lots, 5,102 returns, 1,216 promotions and 1,096 calendar rows.

The RQ1 experiment uses the dedicated complete daily shop--category panel:
32 shops, 16 categories, 512 series and 1,096 daily observations per series,
for 561,152 rows. The panel contains 1,580,361 observed units. The separate
simulated unmet-demand table is not used as observed sales or as empirical
lost-sales ground truth. Raw business records remain in controlled storage;
the public repository contains code, schemas, checksums and aggregate outputs.

\section{Data Validation and Preprocessing}

The ingestion procedure parses the date field, checks required columns and
missing values, rejects duplicate shop--category--date keys, verifies that
units sold are finite and non-negative, and checks that the zero-sales flag
agrees with the observed quantity. It then verifies that every shop--category
series contains the same complete daily calendar. No missing dates are filled
by assumption. The audit found zero duplicate keys, zero missing required
values, zero zero-flag mismatches, and zero missing dates in the supplied
panel. The zero-sales share is 37.5962\%.

The dedicated SKU-level export is not substituted for this complete
shop--category panel. Its absent SKU-date rows require a separate source-aware
decision about assortment, shop opening, product activation, stock-outs, and
export completeness before zero demand can be assigned. This distinction
prevents the conversion of unobserved records into artificial zero labels.

\section{Transaction and Panel Reconciliation}

The 736,683 sales lines join to 365,447 sales orders without missing order
references or shop mismatches. Aggregating source sales lines by
\texttt{shop\_id}, \texttt{sku\_id} and calendar date reproduces all 699,220
rows of the prepared positive-sale SKU table exactly, including 1,580,361
units and the recorded sales value to floating-point precision. This is an
internal reconciliation of the supplied files, not an independent audit of
their real-world origin.

The prepared SKU export contains 32 shops, 480 SKUs and 13,271
shop--SKU combinations with at least one positive-sale record. It contains
only positive-sale days; its \texttt{zero\_day\_interpretation} field warns
that missing SKU dates are not automatically zero demand. The complete
shop--category panel contains five fields:
\texttt{shop\_id}, \texttt{category}, \texttt{date},
\texttt{units\_sold\_observed}, and
\texttt{is\_zero\_observed\_sales}. It has 561,152 rows, 32 shops, 16
categories, 512 series and 1,096 consecutive daily observations per
series. The total is 1,580,361 units and 210,972 rows (37.5962\%) are
zero-sales observations. Aggregating SKU sales to shop--category--date
reproduces all 350,180 positive category-day rows exactly; the remaining
210,972 category rows are explicit zero-sales rows in the complete panel.

\section{Inventory and Procurement Reconciliation}

The inventory ledger contains 13,272 opening movements, 121,107 purchase
movements, 736,683 sale movements, 118,673 expiry-disposal movements and
5,073 damage movements. Every sale movement matches a sales line by order,
shop, SKU, lot and quantity. The 121,107 purchase movement groups match
received purchase-order/SKU quantities; 1,315 purchase lines have no final
received quantity and remain pending at cutoff.

A chronological stock reconstruction identifies 269 negative-balance
movement events across 253 shop--SKU combinations. It also identifies 330
positive-sale days with non-positive reconstructed intraday stock. These
records are quarantined from SKU exposure labels pending correction. Across
the 13,272 inventory-tracked shop--SKU combinations and 1,096 dates, the
ledger reconstructs 13,781,575 fully in-stock days and 764,537 days with
non-positive intraday balance. These are derived diagnostics, not an
independent stock audit. Every zero-sales shop--category day has at least
one recorded SKU with positive reconstructed stock during that day; this
does not rule out item-level stock-outs or unrecorded assortment.

\section{RQ1 Forecasting Pipeline}

For each shop--category series, the feature builder uses only information
strictly before the forecast origin. Features include lags of 1, 7, 14, 28,
and 56 days; rolling mean, standard deviation, sum, and zero-sales ratio
over 7, 14, 28, and 56 days; days since the last positive sale; ADI; positive
demand mean and \(CV^2\); shop and category identifiers; weekday, day,
month, ISO week, and cyclical calendar encodings. No future observed sales
are used as predictors.

Four direct cumulative targets are evaluated: 1, 7, 14, and 28 days.
Development forecast origins are 1 July, 1 August, and 1 September 2025.
The previously inspected fourth-quarter 2025 period is not used for model
selection. Historical training examples are sampled every 28 days; each
training label window must finish before the corresponding validation origin.
A 56-day history is required before a feature row is admitted.

The LightGBM Tweedie candidate uses variance power 1.3, 100 boosting
iterations, learning rate 0.035, 31 leaves, minimum child samples 80,
row and column subsampling 0.85, subsampling frequency 1, L2 regularisation
2.0 and seed 42. The CatBoost Tweedie challenger uses 120 iterations,
depth 6, learning rate 0.04 and L2 regularisation 5. The two-stage CatBoost
hurdle uses a 100-iteration occurrence classifier and a 100-iteration
positive-quantity regressor, depth 5 and learning rate 0.04; the classifier
uses balanced class weights. Its probabilities are not calibrated and its
output is not selected for deployment. Croston--SBA and TSB use
\(\alpha=0.1\) (and \(\beta=0.1\) for TSB). The weekly seasonal-naive
baseline averages up to four previous observations for each matching
weekday and sums them across the horizon. All methods are evaluated on
the same 512 series at each of the three origins, giving 1,536
series-origin forecasts per horizon.

\section{Context-Aware Forecasting Model Development}

The existing pooled LightGBM Tweedie model was retained as the controlled
baseline. The challenger used the same estimator family and settings while
adding cross-series information available before each forecast origin:
shop-level sums and means, category-level sums and means, network totals,
recent shop/category trends and the focal series' share of its shop,
category and network totals. This tests whether related series provide
useful context without introducing future information.

The seven-day comparison uses nine monthly origins from January to September
2025, with all 512 series evaluated at every origin. One-, 14- and 28-day
comparisons use three origins from July to September 2025. Q4 2025, which
had already been inspected, is not used for model selection. Every training
target window ends before its validation origin. Baseline and context
models use the same target, origins and LightGBM settings.

The model remains a shop--category observed-sales forecaster. It does not
convert missing SKU dates to zero or claim to estimate unconstrained latent
demand. Code and aggregate outputs are stored under
\texttt{research/forecasting/context\_aware\_experiment.py} and
\texttt{research/forecasting/results/context\_aware\_v1/}.

\section{Historical Transaction-Feature Ablation}

A separate seven-day ablation tests whether transaction-derived covariates
reduce error beyond the historical demand features. Sales lines are joined
to parent orders and the product master, then aggregated to shop--category--day
for gross value, discount, order count, customer-days and quantity-weighted
unit price. The aggregated quantity reproduces the complete observed
shop--category panel exactly. The extended feature set uses only historical
price, discount, order and customer-activity summaries from the 7-, 28- and
56-day windows before each forecast origin. It does not use future prices,
promotion schedules, inventory outcomes or customer responses.

The ablation compares baseline LightGBM Tweedie with the same model plus
these transaction-derived features at nine monthly origins from January
through September 2025. Each origin covers all 512 series. Training windows
use a 28-day stride and a minimum 56-day history; every training target
window ends before its validation origin. The script and aggregate outputs
are stored in
\texttt{research/forecasting/ablation\_transaction\_features.py} and
\texttt{research/forecasting/results/transaction\_feature\_ablation\_v1/}.
The ablation is development evidence, not an untouched final test.

\section{Software Environment and Reproducibility}

The experiment was executed with Python 3.13.5, pandas 2.2.3, NumPy 2.3.5,
scikit-learn 1.8.0, and LightGBM 4.6.0. The source panel's SHA-256 checksum
is recorded in the accompanying experiment manifest. The implementation
writes the per-origin metrics, aggregate metrics, forecast-level predictions,
demand-regime assignments, and regime-stratified metrics as machine-readable
CSV files. The raw business dataset is retained in its controlled data
location and is not copied into the public source repository.

\section{Current Implementation Boundaries}

The executable experiment remains a shop--category observed-sales forecast.
It does not establish SKU-level latent demand, price or promotion uplift,
or inventory-policy benefit. CatBoost Tweedie and a raw two-stage hurdle
have now been evaluated as challengers; the hurdle probabilities remain
uncalibrated. The inventory ledger has been reconciled internally, but
negative-balance events and positive-sale days with non-positive intraday
stock must be quarantined before SKU exposure labels are used. Calibrated
prediction intervals, hierarchical reconciliation, point-in-time price
and promotion features, and inventory replay remain subsequent tasks.
The package's provenance conflict also prevents real-world effectiveness
claims.

partly reflect cancellation and do not establish equivalent leaf-level
replenishment accuracy. The context model is the leading development
challenger; production selection requires a genuinely untouched future
period or held-out businesses and explicit under-forecast cost evaluation.

\section{Model and Solution Design Decision}

The development evidence supports a conservative two-layer forecasting
design. The forecast layer retains LightGBM Tweedie as the fallback
candidate for shop--category horizons of seven days and longer; CatBoost
Tweedie remains a challenger; the hurdle model remains experimental
until its occurrence probabilities are calibrated and its bias is
controlled; Croston--SBA and TSB remain specialist candidates for
intermittent series. The selection layer compares candidates on identical
origins, horizons and target rows, and selects a specialist only when
at least three origins and at least twenty independent series per
regime per origin are available. Otherwise the pooled fallback is kept.

The next SKU-level design is availability-aware. Missing SKU dates
become zero only when the SKU is active and positive stock is established
throughout the day. Negative-ledger events, stock-out days, unknown
assortment and closed-shop days are censored. The simulated unmet-demand
table is not used as observed target data. Calibrated intervals and
hierarchical reconciliation follow only after the eligible panel and
aggregation identities are frozen. RQ3 inventory-policy replay and RQ4
owner-facing explanation evaluation remain separate experiments with
their own evidence requirements.

\section{Threats to Validity and Summary}

The current result is bounded to a supplied shop--category panel,
three development origins and four cumulative horizons. Its provenance
is unresolved, and the package cannot currently support a claim of
verified real-world SME forecasting accuracy. The previously discussed
83.98\%, 84.04\% and 84.56\% WAPE values are not compared with this
experiment because their exact source code, target, aggregation,
forecast origins and metric denominator have not been reproduced on
identical rows. The Q4-2025 period is not an untouched final test.

The evidence supports an engineering decision for the next development
cycle: keep a pooled fallback, add specialist candidates only behind
validation and support gates, correct the SKU target construction using
inventory exposure, calibrate uncertainty and probabilities, and
evaluate inventory decisions separately from forecast error. No model
is declared a universal winner and no accuracy target is forced.

\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chapter_conclusion}

\section{Research Summary}

This thesis develops and evaluates an integrated SME analytics framework
using the supplied Bangladesh business records. The dataset manifest
identifies the package as real SME sales and real people. Source tables
were internally reconciled: sales lines join to orders, prepared SKU
aggregates reproduce from transaction lines, category totals reproduce
from SKU sales, and inventory sale movements match sales lines. The
inventory audit also identifies negative-balance events and positive-sale
days with non-positive reconstructed stock; these remain quarantined from
SKU exposure labels pending correction.

For RQ1, the complete shop--category panel supports observed-sales
forecasting. A context-aware LightGBM challenger adds historical shop,
category and network aggregates to the baseline features. Across nine
seven-day development origins, pooled WAPE improves from 33.47\% to
32.66\%, with a paired cluster-bootstrap interval supporting lower
development error. However, under-forecast bias increases from -2.02\%
to -6.05\%. The model is therefore a leading development challenger,
not yet a final production selection. The one-, 14- and 28-day comparisons
also show lower pooled WAPE, but use only three origins each.

Prequential interval diagnostics and bottom-up aggregation provide
additional evidence. SKU latent demand, inventory-policy benefit,
customer-action effectiveness and cross-sector transfer remain separate
evaluation tasks. The contribution is an auditable, evidence-aware process
that selects methods against common baselines, preserves temporal validity
and links predictions to feasible business decisions. No accuracy target
is forced and no untested business-impact claim is made.
