\large Small and medium sized enterprises (SMEs) can produce a lot of operational data. But, they have difficulty converting that information to insights and decisions. In this research,We introduce an AI driven business analytics framework, B-SMART (Bangladesh SME Monitoring, Analytics, and Reporting Tool), which was originally designed for retail pharmacies in Bangladesh.


B-SMART unifies business performance evaluation, demand forecasting, customer behaviour analysis, customer inactivity prediction and strategy recommendation in one decision making process. Using a time aware pipeline, the data snapshots are made at scheduled moments and it helps to prevent data leakage. Appropriate validation techniques are applied to evaluate the statistical baselines, pooled multi pharmacy models and shop-adapted models.



The strategy layer translates the findings from the analysis into recommendations. It takes care of factors like Customer consent, Stock availability, Product expiry, Supplier lead time, Storage requirements and Budget. The study is conducted using the design science methodology and the benefits that are assessed are related to the prediction performance, data quality, explainability, robustness, scalability, usability and operational feasibility.

B-SMART is overall intended to offer a turstworthy , reliable and locally relevant decision support system for retail pharmacies in Bangladesh.

\section{Context and Motivation}

Operational data is used for business intelligence and analytics to structure the data for decision-making. Decision makers can explain performance, predict future performance, and select an appropriate response \cite{chen2012business}. In a small or medium enterprise (SME), the journey towards analytics starts with familiar records: invoice lines, product movements, purchase receipts, expenses, payments, and customer visits. These records can shed light on revenue and stock position, but their value becomes limited if they remain on paper ledgers, spreadsheets, and separate applications. The owner might be aware of a sales drop last month but not realize what products, customers, or operating conditions were responsible for the reduction, whether the pattern is likely to persist, or whether an inexpensive course of action should be followed.

This is a more challenging decision than making a dashboard. In retail, to ensure product availability, an owner of a pharmacy should make an early estimate of product demand and order in advance from a supplier. The order should be determined with respect to limited cash, storage capacity, pack multiples, and delivery time. Too little stock will result in stock-outs and lost sales, while too much will lead to high inventory levels and obsolescence. Much of the capital tied up in excess inventory can lead to near expiry or expired stock, which can vary based on batch, remaining shelf life, and temperature sensitive storage. Otherwise, an attractive order may still be possible.

Meanwhile, a once valuable customer may become inactive, or a product may appear profitable because the costs were incompletely recorded. A promotion might lead to an increase in volume without a corresponding increase in contribution. These questions are based on the same operational history but involve different analytical activities within the same decision context.

Hierarchy, intermittency, promotions, and local events are all identified as factors in retail forecasting research. New products, changing assortment, and organisational use of forecasts are also central practical concerns in retail forecasting \cite{fildes2022retail}. Evidence from large forecasting competitions also cautions against assuming that a more complex algorithm is automatically more accurate: combinations, statistical methods, and carefully constructed baselines are still competitive in many series \cite{makridakis2022m5}.

So, the problem investigated in this research is not to put a pretty face on a sales file. It is to construct a defensible path from business events to out-of-time predictions and then from predictions to actionable, explainable decisions.


\section{Bangladesh Pharmacy SME Context}

Based on the research according to  Bangladesh, it is found that the perceived benefit, management and organisational readiness, government support, cost and complexity of the ICTs, organisational context and environment are some of the factors that influence the use of ICTs in SMEs \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital}. Research also shows that mobile financial services need to be more  user friendly. The results mention  that such features are important  to designing a local payment channel .Also  the low friction data entry, as well as the owner readable explanations are essential details that should not just be added on.

This study is based on a definition of population as a functional population. Rather than on a size criterion that might change over time. The set of target pharmacies has stock, sales and purchase records, take branch decisions, product/batch decisions and a limited ability to perform specialist analytics. This can vary based on the research question and could be the pharmacy, branch, stock keeping unit (SKU), batch, customer or historical customer snapshot. Depending on the research question, a customer snapshot is used or not.

The conclusions will include only pharmacies that are listed and evidence gathered. The system is not designed to be used for diagnosis purposes. It does not include information on prescription or dosage


\section{Decision Environment and Stakeholder Requirements}

An SME analytics artifact is used inside an operating system of people, records, cash pledges, and supplier relationships. The owner or manager is the primary decision maker, while the evidence could be produced by sales staff, inventory staff, accountants, and external payment or supply services. A recommendation is reliable only if those roles generate compatible records and the decision-maker can interpret the outcome at the time of action.

The Bangladesh adoption evidence makes the methodological implication clear: perceived value, organisational readiness, cost, and workflow compatibility must be assessed in addition to model accuracy \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital}.

The setting is translated into concrete information requirements in Table~\ref{tab:stakeholder_requirements}. The table is a design analysis of the present thesis. It is not a statement that every participating business has the same staffing structure. One individual can fill more than one role in a micro-enterprise.

There is an important difference between those who create an event, those who validate it, and the person who validates the output and suffers the consequences of a bad decision.

\section{Problem Statement}

The middle problem is the lack of a reliable and convenient link between data from these logs and the analysis of the resulting information. These logs are used for three activities: recording pharmacy operations, extracting predictive insights from the data, and choosing an action that is viable for the business.

Orders can be stored in a transaction system, but even when the data are available, there may be no forecasts. A stand-alone model can report an accuracy score after evaluation, but it can still be untrustworthy because the features may not have existed at the time the prediction was supposed to be made. Its test data may have contained information from the training period, or it may be unable to deliver an output that is related to budget, stock, lead time, and customer consent. A recommendation can therefore be technically correct but not feasible for the business.


The problem is broken down into four related gaps:

\begin{enumerate}[label=G\arabic*.]

\item \textbf{Data foundation gap.} Sales, inventory, purchases, expenses, customers, and payments need a consistent schema, timestamps, provenance, and organisational boundaries before they can support reliable and comparable analytics.

\item \textbf{Prediction-validity gap.} Demand and customer risk models need historical snapshots, chronological or entity-safe splits, training-only transformations, consistent baselines, and metrics suitable for sparse or imbalanced outcomes. Without leakage control, there can be an attractive but false appearance of performance and validity \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

\item \textbf{Prediction-to-action gap.} A forecast or risk probability does not identify what to order, who to reach out to, or whether an action is cost effective. Prediction must therefore be related to costs, constraints, and uncertainty. This represents the shift from predictive to prescriptive analytics \cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}.

\item \textbf{Adoption and accountability gap.} An SME owner requires a short, clear explanation of the reason, confidence indication, and traceable source for a suggestion. A feature-attribution method can explain how features contributed to a model output, but it does not establish causality, nor does it attribute credit or blame for a model's behaviour. Instead of relying solely on human judgement, methods such as SHAP and explanation frameworks provide structured mechanisms for interpreting model behaviour \cite{lundberg2017shap,miller2019explanation}.

\end{enumerate}

This thesis therefore investigates the possibility of an integrated framework that is Bangladesh-aware and can visually represent an auditable retail record, a leakage-controlled forecast, customer insights, and ranked actions based on the operating constraints of an SME. The work does not assume that machine learning can outperform every existing baseline. Finding that a simple rule is more reliable or economical for a particular task is considered a valid result.


\section{Analytical Logic and Claim Boundaries}

The thesis, which is composed of four logical objects, is often lumped together in a single analytics process. Success in one area must therefore be matched by success in another.

This separation also regulates the claims made in the thesis. A dashboard value is \emph{descriptive}: it is supported when the accounting definition and source reconciliation are correct. The likelihood of inactivity indicated by a forecast or inactivity score is \emph{predictive}: it is only available on observations that were not used during model development. A rank recommendation is \emph{prescriptive}: it also calls for specific actions, considers costs, and is planned under constraints and uncertainty \cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}. Several terms are therefore used in a narrow sense. \emph{Demand} refers to the quantity required by customers; recorded sales may be a censored observation of demand when stock is not available. \emph{Future inactivity} refers to no observed demand in a future period and is not assumed to be permanent or, where applicable, a ``forever'' purchase pattern.

Negative and conditional findings are therefore informative within these boundaries. A model may be accurate but not actionable because of a lack of cost data. \section{Initial Solution Concept}

B-SMART the Bangladesh SME Monitoring, Analytics, and Reporting tool is the proposed solution. The first validated application of the framework, is retail pharmacy. The structure is, however, based on SME categories so it can be adapted to an architecture and then later extended and validated on an individual basis for other business categories. Hence, B-SMART is not only an analytical tool, it is a decision support process that links operational data, analysis and viable business actions at different stages of the decision making process.

Pharmacy business events move to a controlled operational layer and validated events are then processed and reconciled. The events confirmed are then converted to analytical snapshot views that fix the time elapsed. When there is enough evidence to perform model comparison, it is done using transparent baselines, a pooled multipharmacy model, and shop adapted candidate models. The selected model forecasts, estimates risks and segments customers for the active strategy layer.

The strategy layer connects these forecasts, risks and segment profiles to viable business strategies. The shop owner states the operational constraints and priorities that are relevant before being ranked and presented as actions. The data cutoff, model or baseline source, uncertainty or error range, supporting documentation, and key supporting factors for each action are displayed. The owner's reaction to the suggestion, along with the subsequent business result, is kept in the system for evaluation and tracking.

The three analytical results are related to the approved scope of the proposed system. Performance evaluation is a summary of the customer behaviour, movement of stocks, and margin and is correlated with revenue generated from reconciled records. Insight extraction predicts future demand, captures significant behavioural segments and provides identification of customer inactivity. These insights are then fed into strategy optimization to generate actionable orders, like changing order sizes and focusing on customer retention, given constraints like available cash, storage, supplier lead time, minimum order quantity, existing inventory and customer permission to be contacted.

\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chapter_conclusion}
This report is the first step towards building a business intelligence system that will leverage AI to analyze the retail pharmacy sector in Bangladesh. It suggests a first implementation and empirical testing of an analytics system for one of its envisioned applications: retail pharmacies in Bangladesh. It is possible that the framework will be expanded in the future to other SME sectors. But each extension would need to be validated and each would have its own data and operation boundaries. The framework will be a portable framework and should not be a one size fits all.

The main problem is not that they don't have a prediction model. The actual issue is the lack of a trustworthy link between the operational data, the analysis with valid time period and the relevant decision of the owner. The usefulness of the forecast is seriously reduced if the source of the data is not identified, if data from the future is used in the test data, or if the recommendation that is made based on the forecast does not consider practical issues like cash availability or stock levels, supplier lead time, or purchasing constraints. Similarly, if a level of risk to the customer isn't calibrated or if the proposed customer contact doesn't include consent, it isn't a product of a decision support process.

Based on the available data the following four main conclusions can be drawn. Second, design factors, which must be considered in the workflow fit, perceived value, cost and local technology adoption (LTA) of secondhand ICTs are the focus of oriented research in Bangladesh \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital,hazra2021mfs}. Secondly, retail inventory research has revealed a set of explicit attributes that must be tackled: intermittency, hierarchy, causality, and interactions between inventory items \cite{fildes2022retail, syntetos2005intermittent, hyndman2011hierarchical, syntetos2010stock}. Third, there are scientific and disciplinary safeguards like leakage control, imbalance aware evaluation, probability calibration, and understandable evidence \cite{kaufman2012leakage,saito2015pr,niculescu2005calibration,lundberg2017shap} that are important. Fourth, there is yet no integration and evaluation of these principles in a bounded and Bangladesh based SME workflow. This is the main research gap which this study seeks to fill.

We propose a framework, called \system{}, to overcome this by constructing a traceable sequence of activities, based on multi pharmacy operational evidence, snapshots of historical events and activities, comparison of these models, estimation of uncertainties, explanation, constraint-aware ranking of the actions in the shop, owner's response, and monitoring of the outcomes of the executed actions. The transparent baseline is the model which the pharmacy should strive to emulate while the pooled model and the eligible shop-adapted model are offered mainly as reference models for comparison.

The framework includes practical considerations that may impact the feasibility of an inventory action, such as buying budget, available inventory, pack size or minimum order amount, supplier lead time, product expiration date, product storage requirements, and customer consent. That is, not a new algorithm, nor the notion that a more complex model will have a better business outcome, but a series of decisions to inform business behavior and an empirical analysis of that series of decisions.

The framework is also intended to allow for continuous operation. Model drift and decisions can be monitored with time, and data and recommendations can be updated as the software is applied in the pharmacy. Continuous operation isn't uncontrolled retraining following each transaction. Any replacement model should be versioned and reviewed histrically with regards to the existing model and existing common standard that was used before the release of the replacement model.

The system is still a business decision support system. Does not provide medical diagnoses, orders drugs or prescribes doses of drugs.

In this study, a testable step is provided in the process of moving to the next level of study  on ethics and multi pharmacy partner data readiness. Future inputs should include inputs from schema, constraint specific to the pharmacy, model transparency and model monitoring, model freezing, held out-pharmacy evaluation, untouched period evaluation, usability tasks, and decision impact analysis. These are key to determining the applicability of the system in real pharmacy environments in practice.

However, at this stage the study will not be able to validate the overall system effectiveness. Then claims should be final consistent with the evidence that was observed. For example, if there is no clear distinction between models or conditions of decision support, it would be wrong to conclude that the increased complexity or explanations were improving the decision-making process. The next should rather be reported as evidence of the limits of the approach being proposed, and to focus the research questions.
