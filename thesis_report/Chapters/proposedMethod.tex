\chapter{Proposed Methodologies}
\label{chapter_methodology}

\section{Research Design}

The thesis follows a design-science and empirical evaluation approach. A
software artifact is designed to solve a practical decision problem, while its
analytical components are tested under reproducible protocols. The research has
four evidence stages: (i) synthetic data for safe development and failure-mode
analysis, (ii) public real data for external method validation, (iii) primary
consenting Bangladeshi SME data for the main claims, and (iv) owner-centered
usability and decision-impact evaluation.

The unit of forecasting is branch--SKU--day or branch--SKU--week, depending on
data density. The unit of customer-risk modelling is a customer snapshot at a
historical cutoff. The unit of strategy evaluation is an eligible action whose
cost, risk and operational feasibility are known.

\section{Research Questions and Hypotheses}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt]
  \item[RQ1.] Do Bangladesh-aware calendar, price, promotion and market features
  improve branch--SKU demand forecasts over seasonal and statistical baselines?
  \item[RQ2.] How accurately and reliably can historical customer snapshots
  predict no repeat purchase in the following 60 or 90 days?
  \item[RQ3.] Does a forecast-driven reorder policy reduce stock-outs and holding
  cost compared with the participating owner's existing policy?
  \item[RQ4.] Do concise Bangla action explanations improve task completion,
  confidence and perceived usability for SME owners?
\end{description}

The associated hypotheses are:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt]
  \item[H1.] A model with local calendar, price, promotion and market features
  produces lower out-of-time WAPE/MASE than its otherwise identical ablation.
  \item[H2.] A temporally evaluated customer-risk model achieves PR-AUC above the
  outcome base rate and lift at 10\% above 1.0 on unseen future snapshots.
  \item[H3.] Under identical demand sequences and constraints, forecast-driven
  replenishment reduces total stock-out plus holding cost relative to the
  current-policy baseline.
  \item[H4.] In a counterbalanced owner study, the explanation condition improves
  task completion or confidence without a harmful increase in completion time.
\end{description}

These hypotheses will be evaluated per business and in aggregate. Failure to
reject a null hypothesis will be reported as a result, not repaired by changing
the metric after inspection.

\section{Target Population and Data Acquisition}

The target population is inventory-based retail SMEs in Bangladesh. The minimum
primary target is three to five businesses from a comparable vertical, with
12--24 months of invoice lines, at least 50,000 lines in total, approximately 500
SKUs and 2,000 pseudonymous customers. A smaller first partner with six months of
records may be used to validate collection and import before the full study.

\section{Ethics, Privacy and Data Governance}

University ethics approval and a signed data-sharing agreement precede primary
collection. Names, phone numbers, e-mail addresses, national IDs, account
numbers and wallet numbers are excluded from research tables. Each organization
receives a different salted pseudonymous customer identifier; any re-identification
mapping remains under the business's control outside the repository.

The agreement records purpose, permitted researchers, retention deadline,
publication form and deletion procedure. Only aggregated or separately reviewed
de-identified outputs may be published. Customer contact recommendations require
recorded consent. Tenant identifiers are enforced in application queries and
foreign keys so one organization's data cannot enter another's model or dashboard.

\section{Formal Problem Formulation}

For business $b$, branch $l$, product $p$, customer $c$ and time $t$, define

\begin{equation}
  D=\{\text{Sales, Inventory, Purchase, Expense, Customer, Payment, Context}\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Demand Prediction}

\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{b,l,p,t+h}=
f(X_{\mathrm{history}},X_{\mathrm{price}},X_{\mathrm{promotion}},
X_{\mathrm{calendar}},X_{\mathrm{market}}),
\label{eq:demand}
\end{equation}
where the output is expected units at horizon $h$. Quantile models or calibrated
residual intervals will provide a plausible demand range, not only a point.

\subsection{Future Customer Inactivity}

\begin{equation}
q_{c,t}=P\!\left(\text{no purchase in }(t,t+H]\mid X_{c,t}\right),
\label{eq:churn}
\end{equation}
where every feature in $X_{c,t}$ is computed using timestamps no later than $t$.

\subsection{Strategy Optimization}

For an eligible action $a\in A$,

\begin{equation}
U(a)=E[\Delta\text{Profit}(a)]-\text{Cost}(a)-\lambda\,\text{Risk}(a),
\label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
subject to budget, storage, current/incoming stock, supplier lead time, minimum
order quantity and customer consent. The system returns the top feasible actions
with model/baseline provenance and supporting factors.

\section{Preprocessing and Historical Feature Construction}

The pipeline validates keys, timestamps, monetary identities and stock movement
reconciliation before modelling. Duplicates and cancellations remain auditable;
they do not silently enter revenue. Missingness is profiled. Imputation,
encoding and scaling are fitted only to training periods or training customers.

Demand features include lagged units, rolling mean/variance, day-of-week, month,
festival indicators, price, discount, stock-out flag, promotion and external
market price where available. Customer snapshots include recency, frequency,
monetary value, order-value statistics, product/category breadth, return rate,
discount behavior, tenure and purchase rate. The outcome window is separated
from the observation window by a purge interval for future-repeat modelling.

\section{Analytical Models}

\subsection{Segmentation and Classification}

K-Means on scaled RFM features discovers candidate behavioral groups. Elbow and
silhouette diagnostics inform $K$, but business interpretability is reported
separately from natural separation. Logistic Regression, Random Forest
\cite{breiman2001rf} and XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost} predict existing labels
only after excluding label-derived CLV and grouping records by customer.

\subsection{Forecasting}

The baseline ladder begins with last-value and seasonal-naive forecasts, followed
by statistical ARIMA/SARIMA, gradient boosting on lagged features, and LSTM only
when sequence volume supports it. This ordering implements evidence from forecast
competitions that complex models do not consistently dominate naive/statistical
benchmarks \cite{makridakis2018m4,makridakis2022m5}.

\subsection{Churn and Return Risk}

Logistic Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost are compared. Churn is a
future-repeat outcome; return risk uses pre-dispatch features only. Class weights
and threshold selection may be tuned on validation data. Final selection uses
PR-AUC, calibration, lift and estimated action value rather than accuracy.

\subsection{Explainability}

SHAP values provide global rankings and local contributions for tree models
\cite{lundberg2017shap,lundberg2020trees}. Explanations are transformed into
short supporting statements. They identify associations used by the model and
are never worded as causal conclusions.

\section{The \system{} Sequential Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\system{} Strategy Recommendation}
\label{alg:bsmart}
\KwIn{Business data $D$, horizon $H$, constraints $C$, model versions $M$, actions $A$}
\KwOut{Ranked actionable recommendations $R$}
Validate schema, timestamps, tenant ownership and missingness\;
Build a time-aware feature snapshot $X_t$\;
Predict SKU demand and uncertainty intervals\;
Predict future customer inactivity probabilities\;
Calculate KPIs and abnormal deviations\;
Generate candidate actions from forecasts, risks and KPIs\;
\ForEach{$a\in A$}{
  Estimate benefit, cost and risk\;
  Check $a$ against constraints $C$\;
  $U(a)\leftarrow E[\Delta\mathrm{Profit}(a)]-\mathrm{Cost}(a)-\lambda\mathrm{Risk}(a)$\;
}
Remove infeasible actions and rank the remainder by $U(a)$\;
Attach explanation, confidence source and supporting evidence\;
Return the top-$K$ actions and record later owner response/outcome\;
\end{algorithm}

For $N$ transaction lines and $P$ candidate actions, validation and feature
aggregation are $O(Nd)$ for $d$ inspected fields, and action filtering/ranking is
$O(P\log P)$. Training cost depends on the selected model: for example, a forest
with $T$ trees has an approximate $O(TN\log N)$ construction cost under balanced
trees. Serving reuses versioned artifacts, so online work is feature retrieval,
model inference and $O(P\log P)$ ranking rather than retraining.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsection{Forecasting Metrics and Splits}

Models are trained on earlier observations and tested on the final untouched
period. Rolling-origin evaluation will be used when data volume allows. For
actual values $y_i$ and forecasts $\hat{y}_i$,

\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\hat{y}_i|,\\
\mathrm{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2},\\
\mathrm{WAPE} &= \frac{\sum_i|y_i-\hat{y}_i|}{\sum_i|y_i|}.
\end{align}

MASE, interval coverage, stock-out units, holding units and total simulated cost
will supplement these measures. Calendar, price/promotion, market context and
business-specific modelling will be removed one group at a time for ablation.

\subsection{Classification and Ranking Metrics}

Customer-risk models report PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier score, calibration plots,
lift at 10\% and expected campaign profit. Segment classifiers report accuracy,
macro-F1, confidence intervals and paired McNemar tests. All entity-level tasks
keep an entity on one side of a split.

\subsection{Clustering Evaluation}

Candidate $K$ values are compared using inertia, silhouette and stability under
resampling. Cluster profiles are then checked for size, RFM differences and
actionability. Commercially named clusters are not treated as natural kinds
when silhouette or stability is weak.

\subsection{Scalability, Usability and Business Impact}

The scalability plan covers 100, 500, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and 1,000,000
synthetic transaction rows, measuring generation, import, preprocessing,
training, prediction latency, API throughput, peak memory, artifact size and
database query time. Market-basket minimum-support tests are deferred until an
association-rule component is implemented; no placeholder values will be used.

Owner usability will measure task completion, completion time, error count,
confidence and a standard usability questionnaire, with and without explanation
cards. Reorder impact will compare identical demand sequences under the existing
policy and \system{} policy, reporting stock-outs, holding cost and total utility.
