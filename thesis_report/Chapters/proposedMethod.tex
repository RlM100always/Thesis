\section{Research Approach}

The thesis will use design science with empirical evaluation. Design science is
appropriate because the research produces an artifact intended to address a
practical organizational problem and evaluates that artifact against explicit
requirements \cite{hevner2004design}. The artifact is the \system{} framework,
including its canonical data design, time-aware analytical pipeline,
constraint-aware recommendation procedure and owner-facing evidence. Empirical
study is required because a plausible architecture alone cannot establish
predictive validity, usability or business value.

The study is organized into four connected phases:

\begin{enumerate}[label=P\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Problem and evidence definition:} establish the target SME
  setting, research questions, literature-derived requirements, permitted
  claims and success measures.
  \item \textbf{Artifact design:} specify the operational schema, historical
  snapshots, candidate analytical methods, strategy constraints, interfaces and
  audit records.
  \item \textbf{Technical evaluation:} test data integrity, prediction,
  calibration, robustness and scalability using strictly separated development,
  public-real and primary-real evidence.
  \item \textbf{Decision and user evaluation:} assess simulated or pilot
  inventory consequences and test whether owners can understand and correctly
  use the recommendations.
\end{enumerate}

The unit of forecasting is a branch--SKU--day or branch--SKU--week, selected
according to data density. The unit of customer-risk modelling is a customer's
feature snapshot at a historical cutoff. The unit of strategy evaluation is an
eligible action evaluated under the same information and constraints as its
comparison policy.

\section{Design Requirements and Acceptance Logic}

The literature and problem formulation are converted into eight requirements.
They are stated before implementation so that the artifact can be evaluated
against a stable purpose rather than judged from whichever functions are easiest
to demonstrate.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2em]
  \item[DR1: Decision relevance.] Every analytical output must name its user,
  decision, unit, horizon and feasible response. An output that has no defined
  decision remains exploratory and cannot be promoted as a recommendation.

  \item[DR2: Operational integrity.] Source events must retain organization
  scope, stable keys, event time, receipt time, status and correction history.
  KPI and model views must be reproducible from those events.

  \item[DR3: Temporal validity.] Features, preprocessing parameters and model
  selection must use only evidence available by the relevant cutoff. Final
  evaluation must be performed once on a period or entity group not used for
  selection \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

  \item[DR4: Proportionate modelling.] Every task begins with a transparent
  baseline. A more complex candidate is accepted only when the available data
  support it and its improvement is material, stable and relevant to the
  decision.

  \item[DR5: Uncertainty and reliability.] Forecast intervals, probability
  calibration, data-quality status and sensitivity to uncertain costs must be
  exposed at the point where they can change an action
  \cite{niculescu2005calibration}.

  \item[DR6: Feasible and responsible action.] Strategy generation must apply
  cash, stock, lead-time, order-size, contact-consent and policy constraints
  before ranking. Infeasible actions must be excluded rather than displayed
  with a low score.

  \item[DR7: Human authority and comprehension.] Recommendations must include a
  concise reason, source and alternative; the owner must be able to inspect,
  reject or defer the action. Explanation quality is assessed through decision
  tasks, not inferred from the presence of an attribution
  \cite{lundberg2017shap,miller2019explanation}.

  \item[DR8: Accountability and reproducibility.] Every released output must
  retain the dataset, snapshot, feature, model or rule, constraint and interface
  versions together with the owner response and any later observable outcome.
\end{description}

Acceptance is conjunctive rather than compensatory. High predictive accuracy
cannot compensate for leakage, privacy failure or an infeasible action. A usable
interface cannot compensate for an unreconciled KPI. Conversely, a candidate
need not be the most complex model to satisfy the artifact requirements. A
simple baseline can be the accepted component when it is temporally valid,
adequate for the decision, more stable and easier to maintain.

The evaluation will classify each requirement as \emph{met}, \emph{partially
met}, \emph{not met} or \emph{not evaluable with the available evidence}. The
last category is important: absence of suitable cost, consent or follow-up data
must narrow the conclusion rather than be converted into an assumed success.
Requirement-level findings will be reported alongside task metrics so that a
single aggregate score cannot hide a critical failure.

\section{Research Questions, Hypotheses and Measures}

The research questions introduced in Chapter~\ref{chapter_introduction} are
operationalized as follows:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2em]
  \item[RQ1 / H1.] A demand model containing local calendar, price, promotion
  and available market-context features will reduce out-of-time WAPE or MASE
  relative to the otherwise identical model without those feature groups and
  relative to a seasonal-naive baseline.
  \item[RQ2 / H2.] A model trained from historical customer snapshots will
  identify future inactivity better than the event base rate, measured by
  PR-AUC, and will achieve Lift@10\% above 1.0 while retaining acceptable
  probability calibration.
  \item[RQ3 / H3.] Under identical demand sequences, costs and constraints, the
  proposed forecast-driven replenishment policy will reduce total stock-out and
  holding cost relative to the participating owner's documented current policy.
  \item[RQ4 / H4.] In counterbalanced decision tasks, an explanation condition
  will improve task correctness or confidence without a practically harmful
  increase in completion time compared with an output-only condition.
\end{description}

\section{Target Population and Sampling Plan}

The target population comprises inventory-based retail SMEs operating in
Bangladesh. To reduce uncontrolled variation, initial recruitment will focus on
three to five businesses from one broadly comparable retail vertical. The
planned analytical target is 12--24 months of records across the partners,
subject to ethics approval and data availability. A first partner with a shorter
history may be used only to test collection, mapping and data-quality rules.

Businesses will be recruited purposively because access to longitudinal invoice
and stock data requires owner agreement. Inclusion requires identifiable dates,
products and quantities and a person able to explain the current replenishment
process. Customer-level analysis additionally requires stable pseudonymous
customer identifiers and sufficient follow-up after each cutoff. A business or
task will be excluded from a particular analysis when these minimum conditions
are absent; it will not be repaired by inventing identifiers or outcomes.

\section{Data-Quality Assessment and Task Eligibility}

Quality assessment will be performed before feature engineering and repeated
after every structural transformation. A dataset-wide average will not be used
as the sole quality claim because it can conceal failure in a field essential
to one task. Instead, a quality profile will be calculated by business, branch,
field, period and analytical task.

For required field $j$ in eligible row set $G$, completeness is

\begin{equation}
C_{j,G}=1-\frac{\sum_{i\in G}\mathbb{I}(x_{ij}\text{ is missing})}{|G|}.
\label{eq:completeness}
\end{equation}

Completeness does not imply validity. Product codes will be tested for referential
integrity; invoice-line totals will be recomputed from quantity, price and
discount; returns and cancellations will be reconciled to their source order
when the identifier exists; and payment totals will be compared with invoice
settlement status. Duplicate detection will distinguish exact repeated rows
from legitimate repeated purchases. Candidate duplicates will be logged and
resolved by a declared rule rather than deleted solely because their values are
similar.

Inventory provides a structural reconciliation test. Let
$I^{\mathrm{open}}_{p,t}$ and $I^{\mathrm{close}}_{p,t}$ denote the recorded
opening and closing balance for product $p$ over interval $t$, and let
$\Delta I_{p,t,m}$ be a signed movement such as receipt, sale, return, damage or
manual correction. The residual

\begin{equation}
r_{p,t}=I^{\mathrm{close}}_{p,t}-I^{\mathrm{open}}_{p,t}
-\sum_m \Delta I_{p,t,m}
\label{eq:stock_reconciliation}
\end{equation}

should be zero when all movements and balances are recorded consistently. A
nonzero residual is not silently forced to zero. Its magnitude, period and
likely source are retained as a quality event, and affected observations are
flagged for sensitivity analysis or exclusion.

Temporal tests will identify events recorded before their source transaction,
receipts before purchase orders, negative or implausible lead times, customer
features after their cutoff and product histories outside the active period.
Event time and ingestion time will be stored separately where available. When
only a date is known, the system will not invent false within-day precision.

Task eligibility is then determined from necessary evidence rather than from a
single arbitrary missing-data threshold. Demand forecasting requires a stable
product-time series and a defensible availability interpretation. Margin KPIs
require cost provenance. Customer inactivity requires stable pseudonymous
identity and sufficient future follow-up. Replenishment evaluation additionally
requires stock or a declared inventory reconstruction, lead-time assumptions
and cost ranges. Owner-facing retention requires consent and a permitted contact
mechanism. A task-business-period combination that fails a necessary condition
is labelled ineligible; the reason becomes part of the audit report.

This gate prevents statistical imputation from creating evidence that the
business never recorded. Imputation remains permissible for a model feature
when its missingness mechanism and decision-time availability are understood,
the transformer is fitted on training data only, and an ablation or sensitivity
check shows whether the choice materially affects results. It is not permissible
to impute identifiers, consent, future outcomes or the ground truth used for the
primary test.

\section{Ethics, Privacy and Governance}

University ethics approval and a signed data-sharing agreement will precede
primary collection. Direct identifiers such as names, phone numbers, e-mail
addresses, national identifiers, account numbers and wallet numbers are not
required for the research tables. Each business will use a separate salted
pseudonymous customer key; any re-identification mapping will remain with the
business and outside the research repository.

The agreement will state research purpose, authorized researchers, storage,
retention deadline, publication form, withdrawal and deletion procedure. Access
will follow least privilege, and published material will use aggregates or
separately reviewed de-identified examples. A retention suggestion will not
authorize contact: customer consent and the participating business's policy are
independent feasibility constraints. Model outputs will support, not replace,
owner decisions.

\section{Formal Problem Formulation}

For business $b$, branch $l$, product $p$, customer $c$ and time $t$, let

\begin{equation}
  D_t=\{\text{Sales, Stock, Purchases, Expenses, Customers, Payments, Context}\}_{\leq t}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Performance Evaluation}

For each KPI $K$, the system returns $K(D_t;w,g)$, where $w$ is the reporting
window and $g$ is the aggregation level. Each value retains numerator,
denominator, filters and data-quality status so that the owner can reproduce it.

For valid invoice lines $i$ in reporting window $W$, net sales will be defined
from the business's documented discount, cancellation and return treatment. A
general form is

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}=\sum_{i\in W}q_i p_i
-\sum_{i\in W}d_i-\sum_{r\in W}v_r,
\label{eq:net_sales}
\end{equation}

where $q_i p_i$ is gross line value, $d_i$ is the recognized discount and
$v_r$ is the value of valid returns or reversals assigned to the window. Gross
margin is reported only when comparable cost evidence exists:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GrossMarginRate}_{W}=
\frac{\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}-\mathrm{COGS}_{W}}{\mathrm{NetSales}_{W}}.
\label{eq:gross_margin}
\end{equation}

If cost is missing for a material share of sold units, the system reports sales
and cost coverage rather than silently interpreting revenue as profit. Average
order value uses eligible completed orders as the denominator; repeat rate
states the customer-identification coverage; stock turnover states both the
cost and average-inventory convention. Period comparisons will use comparable
windows or explicitly label trading-day and closure differences.

Each KPI response consists of a value, definition version, period, aggregation,
coverage indicators and reconciliation status. This structure makes a change in
business-rule definition visible. It also permits the evaluation to separate a
calculation defect from a genuine change in business performance.

\subsection{Demand Prediction}

\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{b,l,p,t+h}=f\!\left(X^{\mathrm{history}}_{t},X^{\mathrm{price}}_{t},
X^{\mathrm{promotion}}_{t},X^{\mathrm{calendar}}_{t:t+h},
X^{\mathrm{market}}_{t}\right),
\label{eq:demand}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}$ is expected demand at horizon $h$. Quantile forecasts or
calibrated residual intervals will represent uncertainty. When product-level
forecasts are aggregated, reconciliation will be considered so SKU, category,
branch and business totals remain coherent \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical}.

\subsection{Future Customer Inactivity}

\begin{equation}
q_{c,t}=P\!\left(\text{no purchase in }(t,t+H]\mid X_{c,t}\right),
\label{eq:churn}
\end{equation}
where $X_{c,t}$ contains only records available by cutoff $t$. Horizon $H$ will
be fixed from business purchase cycles before final evaluation.

\subsection{Strategy Optimization}

For an eligible action $a\in A_t$,

\begin{equation}
U(a\mid D_t)=E[\Delta\mathrm{Profit}(a)]-\mathrm{ActionCost}(a)
-\lambda\,\mathrm{Risk}(a),
\label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
subject to budget, storage, minimum order quantity, supplier lead time,
current/incoming stock and contact consent. This is a transparent operational
objective inspired by the predictive-to-prescriptive transition
\cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}; its terms will be estimated and sensitivity
tested rather than presented as known constants.

For replenishment, let the inventory position immediately before action be

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IP}_{p,t}=\mathrm{OnHand}_{p,t}+\mathrm{OnOrder}_{p,t}
-\mathrm{Backorder}_{p,t}.
\label{eq:inventory_position}
\end{equation}

Let $D_{p,t}^{(L)}$ be demand over the uncertain supplier lead-time horizon and
$Q$ a candidate order quantity. A transparent conditional cost is

\begin{equation}
J_p(Q)=c_o\mathbb{I}(Q>0)
c_hE[(\mathrm{IP}_{p,t}+Q-D_{p,t}^{(L)})^+]
c_sE[(D_{p,t}^{(L)}-\mathrm{IP}_{p,t}-Q)^+],
\label{eq:replenishment_cost}
\end{equation}

where $c_o$, $c_h$ and $c_s$ denote declared ordering, holding and shortage
costs. The feasible set enforces supplier pack size, minimum order, storage,
available cash and any category-level purchasing limit. Equation
\ref{eq:replenishment_cost} is an evaluation model, not a claim that the three
costs are known exactly. Candidate policies will be compared across
owner-reviewed ranges, and the system will withhold a precise savings claim
when the ranking changes materially across plausible values.

Uncertainty enters through the lead-time demand distribution, not through a
single inflated point forecast. When reliable quantiles are available, the
system can evaluate candidate quantities against those quantiles; otherwise it
uses residual or replay-based scenarios from validation periods. If stock-out
censoring prevents credible demand reconstruction, the result is reported as a
policy comparison under observed sales rather than as an estimate of fully
unconstrained demand.

Customer action requires a different logic. The inactivity probability
$q_{c,t}$ in Equation~\ref{eq:churn} can prioritize review, but it does not
estimate the causal effect of contacting customer $c$. A contact action is
eligible only if the customer is contactable under consent and business policy,
the proposed treatment is defined and capacity remains. Expected profit will be
calculated only if a defensible incremental response benefit is available;
otherwise the output is explicitly a risk-ranked contact list. This separation
prevents a predictive probability from being misrepresented as campaign uplift.

The action layer can also decline to recommend. Abstention occurs when required
constraints are missing, uncertainty makes the top alternatives practically
indistinguishable, the predicted benefit does not exceed action cost over the
declared sensitivity range, or the relevant model has failed a monitoring gate.
An abstention with a reason is treated as a valid safety behavior rather than a
system error.

\section{Preprocessing and Historical Snapshots}

The processing order is fixed: validate source and schema; remove or mark exact
duplicates; resolve cancellations and returns; construct the split; fit
imputation, encoding and scaling on training data; build features; train; and
evaluate once on the untouched test. The order prevents statistics from future
periods entering training \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

Demand features may include lags, rolling location and variability, day of week,
month, festival distance, price, discount, known promotion, stock-out indicator
and external market context. Zero demand caused by no sale is distinguished,
when possible, from zero observed sales caused by no stock. Intermittency will
be measured before choosing temporal granularity and candidate methods.

Customer snapshots will include RFM, tenure, order-value statistics, category
breadth, purchase interval, discount behavior and pre-cutoff return rate. The
label window follows the observation window, with a purge interval when feature
construction or delayed events could overlap it. No customer identifier appears
on both sides of an entity-generalization test.

\section{Candidate Methods and Selection Rules}

Method selection follows a baseline ladder, and eligibility depends on evidence
volume and task structure.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Performance:} auditable KPI definitions and rule-based anomaly
  flags establish the descriptive baseline.
  \item \textbf{Demand:} last-value, seasonal-naive, moving-average or
  exponential-smoothing baselines precede ARIMA/SARIMA, Croston-family methods,
  gradient-boosted lag models and, only with adequate sequences, LSTM. Croston
  and the Syntetos--Boylan correction address intermittent demand
  \cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent}.
  \item \textbf{Inactivity:} a recency or base-rate rule is compared with
  Logistic Regression, Random Forest \cite{breiman2001rf} and XGBoost
  \cite{chen2016xgboost}. Class weighting and decision threshold are selected on
  development data.
  \item \textbf{Segmentation:} scaled RFM features are studied with K-Means.
  Candidate $K$ values are judged by inertia, silhouette, resampling stability
  and business interpretability.
  \item \textbf{Explanation:} coefficient/rule explanations are preferred for
  simple models; SHAP supplies model-behavior attributions for tree models
  \cite{lundberg2017shap}. Displayed reasons will remain
  selective and contextual \cite{miller2019explanation}.
\end{itemize}

A complex candidate will be rejected when it lacks sufficient training history,
cannot beat the relevant baseline on development data, produces unstable
probabilities, or adds operational cost without material value. This rule keeps
the proposed system useful when the scientifically honest answer is a simple
forecast or business rule.

\section{The \system{} Sequential Procedure}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\system{} Strategy Recommendation}
\label{alg:bsmart}
\KwIn{Data available at cutoff $D_t$, horizon $H$, constraints $C_t$, validated model/baseline versions $M$, candidate actions $A$}
\KwOut{Ranked feasible recommendations $R_t$}
Verify source, schema, timestamps and organization ownership\;
Create the historical feature snapshot $X_t$\;
Apply transformations fitted on the appropriate training evidence\;
Estimate demand with uncertainty and customer inactivity probabilities\;
Calculate reproducible KPIs and data-quality warnings\;
Generate candidate actions with source and decision-time metadata\;
\ForEach{$a\in A$}{
  Check feasibility under $C_t$\;
  Estimate benefit, cost and uncertainty using declared assumptions\;
  $U(a)\leftarrow E[\Delta\mathrm{Profit}(a)]-\mathrm{Cost}(a)-\lambda\mathrm{Risk}(a)$\;
}
Remove infeasible actions and rank the remainder by $U(a)$\;
Attach concise explanation, uncertainty, cutoff and evidence source\;
Return top-$K$ actions; record owner response and later observable outcome\;
\end{algorithm}

For $N$ invoice lines, $d$ validated fields and $P$ candidate actions, source
validation and feature aggregation are approximately $O(Nd)$ and action ranking
is $O(P\log P)$. Training complexity depends on the selected model and is
measured empirically. Serving reuses versioned artifacts; it must not retrain in
the request path.

\section{Snapshot Lineage and Frozen Evaluation Flow}

The analytical snapshot is the unit that prevents future information from
entering an earlier decision. Figure~\ref{fig:snapshot_flow} distinguishes the
observation window, decision cutoff and outcome window. Raw events remain in
the governed ledger. A snapshot builder selects records available by cutoff
$t$, applies a declared mapping and produces task-specific features. The
snapshot identifier, cutoff, source-manifest hash and feature-definition version
are immutable.

For forecasting, multiple cutoffs form rolling origins. Transformations such as
scalers, encoders, imputers and feature-selection rules are fitted within each
training window and then applied to its validation origin. For customer
inactivity, each snapshot contains behavior up to $t$ and receives a label only
after the full horizon $H$ is observable. A purge interval is inserted when a
delayed return, fulfillment event or rolling feature could cross the boundary.
For entity-generalization analysis, the customer or product grouping is applied
before any learned transformation.

Model development uses training and validation snapshots. The final test
manifest remains sealed until the task definition, eligibility rules, feature
set, candidate models, primary metrics, thresholds and random seeds have been
frozen. If a defect is discovered after opening the test, the correction and
its impact are documented; the same observations cannot be repeatedly reused
as if they were still an untouched test. This rule protects the interpretation
of the final result rather than merely the mechanics of a split.

Outcome records do not flow back into the released model automatically. They
first enter a monitoring store and become eligible for a later version only
after reconciliation, label maturation and repetition of the validation
protocol. This delay prevents an owner response, corrected invoice or partially
observed horizon from changing the evidence behind a historical output.

The action component states exactly what is proposed and what comparator would
otherwise be used. The evidence component provides the cutoff, source and
coverage so the owner can detect stale or incomplete data. The reason component
uses a small number of faithful factors and avoids causal language. Uncertainty
and sensitivity state whether nearby alternatives are practically different.
The feasibility component records every binding constraint. Finally, the
accountability component captures accept, reject, defer or modify together with
an optional owner reason and the later observable outcome.

An explanation is considered successful only when it supports correct use. The
owner study will therefore include cases in which the right decision is to
reject or defer a confident-looking recommendation. This tests calibration of
human reliance, not only whether participants prefer a more detailed screen.
The contract also supports failure analysis: a poor decision can be traced to
the underlying estimate, missing constraint, explanation, stale data or owner
interpretation instead of being attributed vaguely to ``the AI.''

\section{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsection{Forecasting Evaluation}

All forecasting splits preserve time. Candidate methods are selected through
rolling-origin or expanding-window validation, followed by one evaluation on a
final untouched period. For actual values $y_i$ and forecasts $\hat y_i$,

\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\hat y_i|,\\
\mathrm{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2},\\
\mathrm{WAPE} &= \frac{\sum_i|y_i-\hat y_i|}{\sum_i|y_i|}.
\end{align}

MASE will support comparison across scales and avoid some defects of percentage
errors \cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. Prediction-interval coverage and mean
interval width will evaluate uncertainty.
Results will be stratified by demand volume and intermittency rather than hiding
failure on low-volume items in one aggregate.

\subsection{Customer and Segmentation Evaluation}

Inactivity models will report outcome prevalence, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Lift@10\%,
Brier score and reliability plots. The emphasis on PR measures follows their
value under imbalance \cite{saito2015pr}; calibration is assessed separately
because a useful ranking can still produce misleading probabilities
\cite{niculescu2005calibration}. Thresholds and contact fractions are chosen on
validation evidence using declared costs, not adjusted after viewing the test.

Clustering evaluation will report inertia, silhouette, cluster stability,
minimum cluster size and RFM profiles. The final interpretation will be reviewed
with domain participants. A low-separation result will be reported as such; the
existence of clusters will not be treated as proof of natural customer types.

\subsection{Ablation, Robustness and Statistical Analysis}

Planned ablations remove one feature family at a time: local calendar,
price/promotion, market context and customer-history extensions. Additional
comparisons examine global versus business-specific models and real-only versus
clearly controlled augmentation. All paired comparisons use the same test
instances or forecast origins.

Uncertainty will be summarized with paired block bootstrap intervals across
time or business units, as appropriate. Model differences will be reported with
effect size and interval, not only a significance label. Sensitivity analysis
will vary lead time, shortage cost, holding cost, action cost and risk penalty in
Equation~\ref{eq:utility}.

\subsection{Frozen Analysis and Statistical Decision Rules}

For each research question, one primary comparison and its primary metric will
be frozen before the final test is opened. RQ1 uses paired out-of-time forecast
errors at common origins, with WAPE or MASE selected according to denominator
and scale suitability. RQ2 uses PR-AUC as the principal discrimination measure
and treats calibration as a separate acceptance condition. RQ3 compares policy
cost on identical demand paths under the same opening inventory and constraints.
RQ4 compares task correctness between explanation conditions, with completion
time and confidence interpreted jointly rather than as independent proof of
benefit.

The unit of uncertainty will match the dependence structure. Forecast errors
from adjacent dates are not treated as independent rows; block bootstrap
resampling will preserve local temporal dependence. When multiple businesses
are available, firm-level variation will be shown rather than hidden inside a
pooled transaction count. Customer comparisons will respect repeated snapshots
from the same customer, and usability tasks will be paired within participant
when the design permits. Confidence intervals will accompany effect estimates
so that imprecision remains visible.

Practical importance will be declared separately from statistical evidence. A
small average error reduction may be practically relevant for a high-volume
item but irrelevant after order constraints; a change in task time may be
acceptable if correctness improves materially. Cost or effect thresholds will
be defined from the participating business context before final comparison.
Where no defensible threshold exists, the report will present the magnitude and
uncertainty without assigning an arbitrary success label.

Secondary metrics, horizons, segments and feature interactions remain useful
for diagnosis, but they will be labelled secondary or exploratory. If many
related comparisons are inspected, the report will disclose their number and
avoid selecting only favorable cases. Any post-test model or threshold change
creates a new exploratory version; it does not replace the frozen primary
result. This separation keeps model improvement possible without rewriting the
original evidence.

Missing outcomes will be counted and explained. An observation with an
incomplete label horizon cannot be a negative inactivity case. An unknown stock
balance cannot be treated automatically as zero stock. A recommendation without
later outcome may still support usability analysis but cannot support realized
impact. Sensitivity bounds will be used when assumptions can be varied
plausibly; otherwise the corresponding claim will be marked not evaluable.

\subsection{Decision, Usability and Scalability Evaluation}

Replenishment policies will face identical observed or replayed demand paths.
The comparison records stock-out units, average inventory, holding cost,
shortage cost and total cost. Retention ranking will use expected value only
when response benefit, contact cost and consent are available; otherwise it will
remain a prioritization study, not a profit claim.

Owner evaluation will use realistic tasks in a counterbalanced within-subject
design when the sample permits. Measures include task correctness, completion
time, error count, confidence and perceived usability. Participants may explain
their decision in their own words, allowing the study to detect plausible but
misunderstood explanations.

Scalability tests will use generated data only and will remain separate from
accuracy results. Planned sizes are 100, 500, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and
1,000,000 transaction lines. Measures include import and preprocessing time,
training time, prediction latency, API throughput, peak memory, artifact size
and representative query latency. Any association-rule support experiment will
be reported only after the corresponding algorithm exists and has been run.

\subsection{Monitoring Plan}

Monitoring will distinguish schema or data-quality failure, input shift,
calibration change and outcome-performance change. Alerts will not automatically
authorize retraining. A new model must repeat the validation protocol and retain
its training window, features, parameters and comparison result. This follows
the methodology's requirement that adaptation must itself be evaluated before
deployment.

\section{Reproducibility, Audit and Failure Reporting}

Reproducibility will be designed at four levels. \emph{Data reproducibility}
requires a source manifest, checksum, schema version, mapping and exclusion log.
\emph{Analytical reproducibility} requires snapshot identifiers, fitted
transformations, feature definitions, target construction and split manifests.
\emph{Model reproducibility} requires code version, environment, parameters,
random seeds, trained artifact and evaluation command. \emph{Decision
reproducibility} additionally requires constraints, cost assumptions,
candidate-action set, ranking rule and interface version.

Primary partner data cannot be published merely to make the study reproducible.
The reproducibility package will therefore separate restricted evidence from
shareable artifacts. It can publish schemas, data dictionaries, manifest
templates, transformation code, generated test fixtures, configuration files
and commands without releasing identifiers or confidential transactions. A
permitted evaluator with the restricted dataset should be able to regenerate
the corresponding snapshot and result; an external reader should be able to
inspect the procedure and run the software on a documented public or generated
fixture.

Every experiment will create a machine-readable run record containing the
research question, dataset and snapshot hashes, training and test windows,
grouping keys, feature version, candidate and baseline versions, parameters,
metrics, warnings, start and finish times, and output artifact hashes. Manual
spreadsheet corrections will not occur outside this record. If an owner supplies
a correction, it enters as a dated source or mapping revision and triggers only
the affected downstream views.

Failure reporting is part of the scientific output. Data eligibility failure,
training failure, numerical instability, non-convergence, resource exhaustion,
constraint infeasibility and evaluation failure will have distinct statuses.
The report will give the denominator of attempted cases and will not compute an
average only over successful model runs without disclosure. A method that fails
on sparse or short series is therefore compared as deployed, not only on the
subset where it happens to return a prediction.

Auditability also applies to the owner interface. Each recommendation receives
an identifier linking its evidence contract, displayed text, owner response and
later outcome. If a recommendation changes after data correction or model
replacement, the earlier record remains immutable and the successor points to
it. This supports accountability without implying that the model, researcher or
owner alone caused the eventual outcome.

The final research package will include a claim-to-evidence index. Each central
claim will identify the relevant research question, figure or table, run record
and evidence tier. Claims supported only by public, generated, simulated or
small-sample evidence will remain labelled accordingly. This index provides a
final check that polished presentation has not expanded the scope of what was
actually evaluated.

\section{Expected Methodological Outputs}

At completion, the methodology is expected to yield a documented canonical
schema and data dictionary, partner data protocol, versioned feature snapshots,
baseline and candidate model specifications, a constraint-aware recommendation
procedure, a reproducible evaluation package and an owner-study instrument.
Implementation detail, observed numerical results and conclusions will be added
to the reserved chapters only after the corresponding evidence is available.
