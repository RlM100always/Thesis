\section{Background Study}

\subsection{Business Intelligence and Decision Support}

Business intelligence (BI) is the set of data, infrastructure, analytical
methods and organizational practices used to improve decisions. Its evolution
from structured enterprise reporting toward web, mobile and sensor analytics is
described by Chen, Chiang and Storey \cite{chen2012business}. In this thesis,
\emph{descriptive} analytics summarizes revenue, margin and stock;
\emph{predictive} analytics estimates future demand or customer inactivity; and
\emph{prescriptive} analytics ranks feasible actions.

Operational and analytical functions must be connected but separately governed.
An operational transaction requires atomic consistency and an audit trail. A
model requires a time-frozen feature view, a reproducible split and versioned
artifacts. \system{} connects the two through a canonical export instead of
training directly on mutable application tables.

\subsection{Bangladeshi SME and Payment Context}

This study uses the term SME for the retail-oriented subset of cottage, micro,
small and medium enterprises (CMSMEs). Exact regulatory thresholds may change;
the operational research population is therefore defined functionally: an
inventory-holding Bangladeshi retailer with branch, SKU, sales, purchase and
payment records, limited analytics staff and owner-managed decisions.

Bangladesh-specific evidence indicates that the perceived advantage, cost,
organizational support, infrastructure and skills surrounding a technology
affect adoption \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital}. Mobile payments are not
an optional integration in this context. SME adoption research identifies
performance expectancy, effort expectancy and cost as significant
\cite{rahman2023mobile}, while qualitative MFS research emphasizes accessibility,
mobility and self-service \cite{hazra2021mfs}. The data model consequently treats
cash, bKash, Nagad, Bangla QR, card and due settlement as first-class choices.

\subsection{Customer Analytics: RFM, CLV and Segmentation}

RFM represents a customer by recency $R$ (how recently the customer purchased),
frequency $F$ (how often) and monetary value $M$ (how much). These behavioral
features are inexpensive to derive from invoices and often more actionable than
demographic attributes. CLV extends the view toward expected future customer
value; Fader, Hardie and Lee show that equal RFM scores need not imply equal
future value and formalize the relationship using iso-value curves
\cite{fader2005rfm}.

Supervised segmentation predicts a pre-existing business label. Unsupervised
segmentation asks whether coherent groups arise from behavior without such a
label. K-Means minimizes within-cluster squared distance,

\begin{equation}
  \underset{\{C_k,\mu_k\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\operatorname{minimize}}
  \sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{x_i\in C_k}\lVert x_i-\mu_k\rVert_2^2.
  \label{eq:kmeans}
\end{equation}

Because a clustering algorithm always returns clusters, internal validation is
essential. The silhouette coefficient compares cohesion with the closest other
cluster \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. A low value should be interpreted as
overlap or continuum, not concealed behind attractive segment names.

\subsection{Demand Forecasting}

A demand forecast estimates units sold at a future horizon. ARIMA models linear
dependence after differencing; seasonal-naive forecasting repeats the
corresponding prior-season value; tree ensembles learn nonlinear relations among
lags, calendar, price and promotion variables. LSTM networks use gates to retain
or discard state over long sequences \cite{hochreiter1997lstm}, but the extra
capacity is not automatically beneficial when a shop has only a short history.

Forecasts must be evaluated chronologically. RMSE emphasizes large errors, MAE
preserves the target unit, WAPE aggregates absolute error relative to aggregate
demand, and MASE compares error with a naive scale. Hyndman and Koehler warn that
percentage metrics can be undefined or unstable around zero and recommend scaled
errors for cross-series comparison \cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. Therefore, final
SKU evaluation will report WAPE/MASE with baseline-relative and business-cost
measures rather than one convenient metric.

\subsection{Churn, Future Repeat and Returns}

In a non-contractual retail setting, churn is not an observed cancellation. It
is defined relative to a cutoff $t$: a customer has no repeat purchase in the
future interval $(t,t+H]$. Features must be frozen at $t$ and the label window
must not overlap training and test periods. Returns are similarly predicted only
from information known before dispatch; satisfaction recorded after delivery is
an outcome, not a valid feature.

Both problems can be imbalanced. A classifier that predicts the majority class
may show high accuracy while identifying no useful customer. Precision--recall
curves, lift, calibration and expected campaign or return cost are more useful
than accuracy alone \cite{saito2015pr,verbeke2012profit}.

\subsection{Explainability, Leakage and Model Governance}

SHAP attributes the difference between a model output and a reference value to
input features using a cooperative-game formulation \cite{lundberg2017shap}.
TreeExplainer provides efficient exact or consistent explanations for common
tree ensembles and permits aggregation from local to global behavior
\cite{lundberg2020trees}. An explanation does not prove causality or fairness;
it states how a fitted model used the encoded input.

Data leakage occurs when the training process receives information that would
not legitimately be available at prediction time \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.
Leakage may enter through a target-derived field, repeated entity in multiple
splits, post-outcome feature or transformation fitted on all data. \system{}
therefore versions features, separates customers or time periods, purges outcome
horizons and fits preprocessing on training data only.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature_review}

The following review contains twenty-two core papers, exceeding the minimum of
twenty requested for the present stage. Each entry states its problem, context,
method, main contribution and relevance or limitation.

\subsection{Bangladesh and Business Analytics Foundations}

\paragraph{Chen, Chiang and Storey (2012).}
The paper organizes BI research into successive generations spanning structured
enterprise data, web intelligence and mobile/sensor contexts
\cite{chen2012business}. It synthesizes more than a decade of publications and
identifies research opportunities rather than evaluating one SME implementation.
It motivates the breadth of \system{}, but leaves local adoption, operational
integration and field impact to domain-specific studies.

\paragraph{Hoque et al. (2016).}
This Bangladesh case study investigates the use, impact and adoption of ICT in
rural SMEs \cite{hoque2016ict}. It identifies factors that influence adoption in
a context underrepresented by mainstream enterprise-systems research. Its
contribution is contextual rather than predictive modelling; it supports a
low-friction local product design but does not provide retail transaction data.

\paragraph{Shahadat et al. (2023).}
Using a structured survey of 535 SME managers and PLS-SEM, the authors evaluate
technology, organization and environment factors affecting digital adoption
\cite{shahadat2023digital}. Relative advantage, cost, management support,
competitive pressure and government support emerge as important. Survey intent
does not demonstrate forecasting accuracy, so \system{} complements it with
transaction-level evaluation.

\paragraph{Rahman and Islam (2023).}
The authors study mobile-payment adoption among 200 Bangladeshi SMEs using an
extended UTAUT model and PLS-SEM \cite{rahman2023mobile}. Performance and effort
expectancy, social influence, cost and voluntariness significantly influence
behavioral intention. The study justifies native local-payment workflows, but it
does not address analytics, inventory or longitudinal business outcomes.

\paragraph{Hazra and Priyo (2021).}
This qualitative study uses affordance theory and interviews to explain how
Bangladeshi users achieve access, mobility, self-control and disintermediation
through mobile financial services \cite{hazra2021mfs}. It contributes rich usage
context that purely technical studies omit. Its user-level qualitative setting
does not quantify SME model performance, but it informs the system's payment and
usability assumptions.

\subsection{Customer Analytics and Segmentation}

\paragraph{Ngai, Xiu and Chau (2009).}
The authors screen 900 papers and classify 87 CRM data-mining studies by customer
lifecycle dimension and analytical function \cite{ngai2009crm}. Classification
and association are prominent, and retention receives substantial attention.
The review provides a taxonomy, but predates current explainability and temporal
leakage practice; \system{} joins several functions in one governed pipeline.

\paragraph{Fader, Hardie and Lee (2005).}
The study links RFM behavior to CLV through Pareto/NBD and gamma-gamma components
and validates the approach on CDNOW customers \cite{fader2005rfm}. Iso-value
curves demonstrate how different transaction histories can imply comparable
future value. The method is principled but assumes a non-contractual purchasing
process and does not solve operational inventory decisions.

\paragraph{Kim et al. (2006).}
Using six months of Korean wireless-service data covering 16,384 customers, the
paper develops customer-value dimensions and segment-specific strategies
\cite{kim2006clv}. It shows how current value, potential value and loyalty can
support differentiated action. The sector and contractual relationship differ
from Bangladeshi retail, limiting direct transfer of labels and features.

\paragraph{Cheng and Chen (2009).}
This work combines quantitative RFM attributes, K-Means and rough-set theory to
classify customer value \cite{cheng2009rfm}. It explores three, five and seven
value classes and extracts interpretable decision rules. The contribution is a
segmentation procedure, while business constraints, temporal validation and
post-deployment monitoring remain outside its scope.

\subsection{Predictive Models and Forecasting}

\paragraph{Breiman (2001).}
Random Forests combine decorrelated tree predictors constructed from bootstrap
samples and random feature subsets \cite{breiman2001rf}. The method offers
classification, regression, internal error estimation and variable importance,
with robustness to noise. It is a strong tabular baseline, though raw importance
does not explain an individual business recommendation.

\paragraph{Chen and Guestrin (2016).}
XGBoost introduces a scalable regularized tree-boosting system with sparsity-aware
learning and efficient approximate split finding \cite{chen2016xgboost}. The
paper reports strong performance across large benchmark tasks. Its engineering
strength does not remove the need for leakage-safe data or calibration; in this
thesis it is compared with simpler models rather than assumed to win.

\paragraph{Hochreiter and Schmidhuber (1997).}
The LSTM architecture addresses decaying error flow in recurrent learning through
gated memory cells \cite{hochreiter1997lstm}. Experiments on artificial
long-lag tasks show that it learns dependencies missed by earlier recurrent
methods. Those results do not guarantee accuracy on a 48-month retail series,
which motivates the thesis's data-volume rule and naive baseline.

\paragraph{Carbonneau, Laframboise and Vahidov (2008).}
The authors compare recurrent networks, neural networks and support-vector
machines with naive, moving-average and regression forecasts on simulated supply
chains and actual Canadian foundry orders \cite{carbonneau2008supply}. Advanced
models perform well, but their advantage over regression is not statistically
significant. This directly supports disciplined baseline comparison in small or
noisy supply-chain data.

\paragraph{Hyndman and Koehler (2006).}
This paper evaluates common univariate forecast-accuracy measures and identifies
degenerate behavior in several percentage formulations
\cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. It proposes MASE as a scale-free alternative suitable
across series. The work concerns measurement rather than retail decisions; the
present thesis adds WAPE, prediction intervals and inventory cost.

\paragraph{Makridakis, Spiliotis and Assimakopoulos (2018).}
The M4 competition evaluates point and interval forecasts across 100,000 series
\cite{makridakis2018m4}. Combinations and hybrid methods dominate, while pure ML
entries often fail to beat statistical benchmarks. The heterogeneous series are
not Bangladesh-specific, but the result is strong evidence against equating
model complexity with forecasting quality.

\paragraph{Makridakis, Spiliotis and Assimakopoulos (2022).}
The M5 competition focuses on 42,840 hierarchical Walmart retail series and
includes explanatory variables and uncertainty forecasts
\cite{makridakis2022m5}. It establishes a realistic large-scale retail benchmark
and highlights hierarchy, intermittency and uncertainty. Walmart's volume and
infrastructure differ sharply from small Bangladeshi shops, leaving a need for
low-data, local validation.

\subsection{Churn, Evaluation Integrity and Explainability}

\paragraph{Burez and Van den Poel (2009).}
The study treats customer churn as an imbalanced classification problem and
compares under-sampling, gradient boosting and weighted Random Forests using AUC
and lift \cite{burez2009imbalance}. It demonstrates that imbalance treatment and
metric choice affect conclusions. Its service-industry setting motivates, but
does not define, non-contractual retail inactivity.

\paragraph{Verbeke et al. (2012).}
The authors argue that statistical discrimination alone can select a financially
inferior churn model and propose a maximum-profit measure
\cite{verbeke2012profit}. Telecommunications datasets show gains from choosing
both the model and contact fraction by expected profit. \system{} adopts the
decision focus, while requiring SME-specific action cost and consent estimates.

\paragraph{Saito and Rehmsmeier (2015).}
Through theoretical and empirical comparisons, this paper shows why
precision--recall plots reveal performance differences hidden by ROC plots on
imbalanced data \cite{saito2015pr}. Its examples are not retail-specific, but the
metric lesson is general. Accordingly, churn and return experiments report
PR-AUC, base rate and lift rather than accuracy alone.

\paragraph{Kaufman et al. (2012).}
The paper formalizes leakage as target information that is illegitimately
available to a data-mining model and discusses detection and avoidance
\cite{kaufman2012leakage}. Competition and industrial examples show that leakage
can produce convincing but invalid scores. It directly explains the thesis's CLV,
customer-split and post-satisfaction ablations.

\paragraph{Lundberg and Lee (2017).}
The authors unify several additive feature-attribution methods and introduce SHAP
values with consistency properties \cite{lundberg2017shap}. The framework
supports local explanations for complex models and connects them to global
patterns. SHAP is descriptive of a model rather than causal, so \system{} presents
it as supporting evidence rather than a reason to automate an action.

\paragraph{Lundberg et al. (2020).}
This work develops efficient explanation methods for tree models and shows how
many local explanations reveal global model structure \cite{lundberg2020trees}.
It makes XGBoost and Random Forest behavior more inspectable at serving time. The
method still inherits biases and leakage from the fitted model, which is why
feature/time audits precede explanation in the proposed framework.

\section{Research Gap and Positioning}

Prior research establishes the value of BI, RFM/CLV, tree ensembles, temporal
forecasting, churn ranking and explainability. Bangladesh-focused adoption work
explains why cost, usability, local payments and organizational readiness matter.
However, no reviewed work jointly provides a Bangladesh-oriented SME operations
system, leakage-safe temporal models, constraint-aware action ranking, local
explanations, transparent evidence tiers and measured owner outcomes.

\system{} is positioned in this intersection. Its novelty is the governed
sequence from business event to evidence-backed action, not a new classifier.
The current prototype validates feasibility and exposes methodological failure
modes. The final thesis must still demonstrate transportability and usefulness
with consenting Bangladeshi SMEs before making claims about real business impact.
