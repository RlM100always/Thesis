\chapter{Background Study and Related Work}
\label{chapter_backStudy}

\section{Background Study}

\subsection{Business Intelligence and the Decision Continuum}

Business intelligence (BI) combines data, infrastructure, analytical methods
and organizational practice to improve decisions. Chen, Chiang and Storey trace
its development from structured enterprise reporting toward web, mobile and
sensor-rich analytics \cite{chen2012business}. This thesis adopts a three-stage
decision continuum. \emph{Descriptive analytics} establishes what happened and
where; \emph{predictive analytics} estimates what may happen next; and
\emph{prescriptive analytics} compares feasible responses. The stages are
dependent: an action recommendation is only as credible as the prediction that
supports it, and a prediction is only as credible as the operational data from
which it was derived.

Operational and analytical workloads also have different integrity needs. A
sale requires a consistent stock and payment update, whereas a model requires a
historical feature view whose values do not change after evaluation. The
proposed framework therefore separates the transaction record from versioned
analytical snapshots. It retains the relationship between them through source
identifiers, timestamps and transformation metadata.

\subsection{SME Digitalization in Bangladesh}

Technology adoption is not determined by model accuracy alone. A mixed-method
case study of rural Bangladeshi SMEs identifies awareness of benefits,
government support, top-management support and financial support as important
ICT-adoption determinants \cite{hoque2016ict}. A later survey of 535 SME
managers combines the technology--organization--environment framework with
diffusion-of-innovation constructs; relative advantage, complexity, perceived
cost, management factors, competitive pressure and government support are among
the significant determinants reported \cite{shahadat2023digital}. These studies
justify attention to cost, workflow fit and organizational readiness, although
neither evaluates a retail prediction system.

Mobile financial services are part of this operating context. Interview-based
research in Bangladesh identifies accessibility, spatial and temporal mobility,
self-control and related affordances in everyday MFS use
\cite{hazra2021mfs}. Consequently, the proposed data model treats payment mode
and settlement status as ordinary business fields. The research does not infer
that a particular provider causes better performance; it asks whether locally
recognizable records and explanations make the analytical workflow more usable.

\subsection{Performance Evaluation and Data Foundations}

Performance evaluation begins with well-defined measures. Revenue, gross
margin, average order value, stock turnover, return rate and customer-repeat
rate must each specify a time window, aggregation level and treatment of
cancellations, refunds and missing costs. A dashboard value without these
definitions can disagree with the accounting record or compare unlike periods.
The canonical layer therefore preserves invoice lines and stock movements while
deriving analytical measures in documented views.

Data provenance answers where a record came from, when it was received and how
it was transformed. For primary SME data, provenance must be joined with
organization isolation and pseudonymous customer identifiers. For public data,
the license, original context and permissible inference must be recorded. A
foreign transaction dataset can test whether code handles real invoices but
cannot establish Bangladesh-specific customer behavior.

\subsection{Data Quality, Provenance and Temporal Semantics}

Data quality is not a single percentage. For this study it has at least five
dimensions. \emph{Syntactic validity} asks whether a value has the expected
type, range and format. \emph{Entity integrity} asks whether invoices, products,
customers and branches have stable keys and valid relationships. \emph{Temporal
integrity} asks whether the event, correction, receipt and availability times
are plausible and ordered. \emph{Reconciliation} asks whether line amounts,
payments, returns and stock movements agree under declared business rules.
\emph{Decision adequacy} asks whether the fields needed for a particular
analysis are sufficiently complete at its decision cutoff. A file may score
well on simple completeness while still be unusable for a margin analysis
because unit cost is absent or for a customer study because identities are not
stable.

This distinction matters because operational data are revised. A cancelled
invoice may remain in an export, a return may arrive days after the sale, and a
late stock adjustment may change the apparent availability history. Replacing
the original value destroys the evidence needed to reconstruct what the system
knew. The stronger design is append-aware: retain the source event, record the
correction or status transition and build a versioned analytical view for a
declared cutoff. This implements the BI principle that analytical value depends
on an intelligible relationship between data, infrastructure and organizational
use \cite{chen2012business}.

Missingness must also be interpreted rather than automatically filled. Missing
customer identity may mean an anonymous cash sale, not a failed import. A
missing unit cost may make gross-margin analysis ineligible while leaving unit
demand forecasting usable. A zero sale may represent zero demand, a closed
shop, a missing file or an unobserved stock-out. These states have different
meanings and should be separated whenever the source supports the distinction.
Consequently, the proposed framework assigns eligibility at the level of a
research task, business and period instead of declaring an entire dataset
universally ``clean'' or ``dirty.''

Provenance completes this quality model. Each derived field should identify its
source columns, transformation version and cutoff; each reported result should
identify the dataset manifest, split and model or rule version that produced
it. This lineage does not guarantee correctness, but it makes errors auditable
and results reproducible. It also prevents a public or generated dataset from
quietly inheriting the claim status of primary Bangladesh SME evidence.

\subsection{Customer Analytics: RFM, CLV and Segmentation}

RFM summarizes \emph{recency}, \emph{frequency} and \emph{monetary value} from
a customer's transaction history. These features are inexpensive to derive and
retain a clear behavioral interpretation. Fader, Hardie and Lee connect RFM to
customer lifetime value (CLV) through Pareto/NBD and gamma--gamma components and
show how different histories can have similar expected value
\cite{fader2005rfm}. CLV is useful as an outcome or planning quantity, but a
field used to define a segment cannot also be admitted as an independent
predictor of that same segment.

Unsupervised segmentation asks whether coherent behavioral groups emerge
without an existing label. For K-Means, the objective is

\begin{equation}
  \underset{\{C_k,\mu_k\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\operatorname{minimize}}
  \sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{x_i\in C_k}\lVert x_i-\mu_k\rVert_2^2.
  \label{eq:kmeans}
\end{equation}

Because the procedure returns clusters even when the data form a continuum,
cluster number and interpretation require validation. The silhouette
coefficient compares within-cluster cohesion with separation from the nearest
other cluster \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. Stability under resampling and
business usefulness are separate tests; an attractive label such as ``loyal''
does not establish that a cluster is statistically stable or actionable.

\subsection{Retail Demand Forecasting}

A demand forecast estimates the units required for a defined product, location
and future horizon. Retail series may be affected by price, promotion,
stock-outs, festivals, assortment changes and aggregation across products or
branches. Fildes, Ma and Kolassa emphasize that retail forecasting practice
cannot be reduced to a single algorithm because data hierarchy, operational
process and forecast use matter alongside statistical accuracy
\cite{fildes2022retail}.

Intermittent SKU demand contains many zero periods separated by nonzero demand.
Croston's method separately smooths nonzero size and the interval between
demands \cite{croston1972intermittent}; Syntetos and Boylan analyze bias and
accuracy in intermittent-demand estimates \cite{syntetos2005intermittent}.
These works motivate an intermittent-demand baseline instead of forcing every
SKU through a dense-series model. At the same time, forecasts must reconcile
across product, category, branch and business totals. Optimal-combination
reconciliation offers a principled way to produce coherent hierarchical
forecasts \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical}.

Model complexity should follow the evidence. ARIMA-family models represent
serial dependence after transformation; tree ensembles can learn nonlinear
effects from lagged, calendar and price variables; and sequence models use
ordered histories. Random Forest and XGBoost provide strong tabular comparators
\cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}. The M5 retail competition demonstrates the
importance of large-scale, hierarchical and uncertainty-aware comparison
\cite{makridakis2022m5}. A seasonal-naive forecast is therefore a required
benchmark, not a ceremonial baseline.

Forecast accuracy and inventory usefulness are related but not identical.
Hyndman and Koehler demonstrate defects in common percentage errors and propose
MASE for scale-free comparison \cite{hyndman2006accuracy}. Syntetos and
colleagues connect forecast selection with stock-control performance in a
wholesaling study \cite{syntetos2010stock}. The methodology accordingly reports
point error, uncertainty and simulated stock-out/holding cost rather than
selecting a method from one convenient metric.

\subsection{Forecastability, Demand Regimes and Decision Loss}

The forecasting task changes with the information content of a series. A
high-volume staple with repeated weekly behavior offers many observations of
level and seasonality. A slow-moving spare item may contain mostly zero periods,
while a newly introduced product has little or no direct history. Pooling these
cases under one algorithm and one average score can hide systematic failure.
The literature on intermittent demand therefore supports a regime-aware
comparison in which zero frequency, nonzero-demand variability and available
history influence method eligibility \cite{croston1972intermittent,
syntetos2005intermittent}.

Temporal aggregation creates a further trade-off. Daily data preserve the
timing needed for short-horizon ordering but may be too sparse for stable model
estimation. Weekly aggregation reduces sparsity but may conceal festival peaks,
stock-out intervals and short lead-time decisions. Product aggregation provides
more signal, yet a category forecast cannot directly determine which SKU to
reorder. Hierarchical reconciliation addresses coherence across aggregation
levels \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical}; it does not remove the need to select a
granularity that matches the operational decision.

Observed sales are also an imperfect target when availability is constrained.
If an item is out of stock, a zero or low recorded sale does not establish that
customers wanted zero units. Training on such periods without a stock indicator
can teach a model that constrained supply is low demand. The appropriate
response depends on evidence: flag and stratify censored periods when stock
history exists, conduct sensitivity analysis when it is incomplete, and avoid
claiming unconstrained demand when availability cannot be reconstructed.

Forecast errors have asymmetric decision consequences. Under-forecasting can
produce shortage or lost-sales cost; over-forecasting can create holding,
expiry or cash-opportunity cost. The best model by MAE need not minimize these
consequences, particularly when errors occur on different products or at
different times. This is why scale-aware error measures, prediction intervals
and identical-demand-path inventory simulation serve complementary purposes
\cite{hyndman2006accuracy,syntetos2010stock}. Model comparison remains
prediction-focused until cost assumptions are declared; only then can a
decision-oriented comparison be defended.

The review consequently rejects three shortcuts: choosing a deep model before
measuring series structure, selecting from a single percentage error, and
treating a forecast competition result as proof of value in a small local
retailer. M5 provides strong evidence about comparative retail forecasting at
scale \cite{makridakis2022m5}; the present task is to test which parts transfer
under substantially different data volume, infrastructure and decision cost.

\subsection{Customer Inactivity, Class Imbalance and Calibration}

In a subscription service, churn may be an observed cancellation. In
non-contractual retail it must be defined at a cutoff $t$: no repeat purchase
during a fixed interval $(t,t+H]$. Every feature must be known by $t$, and the
future label window must not contaminate training or validation. This definition
turns an ambiguous business word into a reproducible prediction task.

The positive class may be rare or change across businesses. Saito and
Rehmsmeier explain why precision--recall analysis is more revealing than ROC
analysis for imbalanced binary tasks \cite{saito2015pr}. Model comparison must
therefore report prevalence and ranking quality before action cost and benefit
are considered in the prescriptive layer.

Ranking is not enough when the output is used in a decision calculation. A
probability of $0.7$ should correspond, approximately, to the event frequency
among comparable cases. Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana compare probability
estimates and post-hoc calibration methods for supervised learners
\cite{niculescu2005calibration}. The proposed framework therefore reports Brier
score and calibration in addition to PR-AUC, ROC-AUC and top-fraction lift.

\subsection{From Customer Score to Responsible Retention Decision}

A customer-risk model creates a ranking or probability, not permission to act.
For a fixed contact capacity, ranking quality determines whether the selected
fraction contains more future inactive customers than an untargeted policy.
For an expected-value calculation, calibration becomes essential because the
probability is multiplied by uncertain benefit and cost. The operational
threshold should therefore be selected from validation data using the declared
contact capacity or utility assumptions, never by inspecting the final test.

The customer record creates additional ambiguity. Frequency and monetary value
can reflect opportunity to visit, household purchasing, shared identifiers,
returns or discount-seeking behavior; none alone proves loyalty or future value.
Fader, Hardie and Lee show why the joint transaction history carries more
information than a single RFM label \cite{fader2005rfm}. Segmentation can help
describe patterns, but assigning a persuasive segment name is an interpretive
step that requires stability checks and owner review, not merely a clustering
output \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}.

Responsible use adds an independent feasibility gate. A high inactivity score
does not justify contact when consent, contact policy or an appropriate offer is
absent. Nor should an attribution such as ``long recency increased risk'' be
restated as a causal claim that a specific message will retain the customer.
The thesis therefore evaluates inactivity prediction, probability reliability,
contact eligibility and later response as separate links. This structure makes
it possible for the model to be scientifically informative even when the
available evidence is insufficient for a profit or retention-effect claim.

\subsection{Leakage, Explainability and Change Over Time}

Data leakage arises when training has access to information that would not
legitimately exist at prediction time. It may enter through a target-derived
field, a post-outcome variable, repeated customers in both sides of a split or a
transform fitted before the split \cite{kaufman2012leakage}. Leakage control is
therefore a property of the entire data pipeline, not a model option. A clear
prediction time, feature availability table and split policy precede training.

SHAP represents a prediction as additive feature contributions relative to a
reference value and supports local and global inspection
\cite{lundberg2017shap}. Such an attribution
describes how a fitted model used encoded features. Miller's synthesis of social
science research emphasizes that useful explanations are selective, contextual
and often contrastive \cite{miller2019explanation}. Thus a recommendation card
should answer ``why this action now?'' in a small number of decision-relevant
factors, while preserving access to fuller evidence and avoiding causal wording.

Business conditions also change. Retail monitoring must distinguish data-quality
failure, input-distribution shift and outcome-performance decay. A retraining
schedule alone is not evidence that a model remains valid; every replacement
model must repeat the declared validation protocol.

\subsection{From Prediction to Constrained Action}

Prescriptive analytics uses predictions within an explicit decision problem.
Bertsimas and Kallus formulate data-driven prescriptions in which auxiliary
observations inform conditional stochastic optimization
\cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}. The present thesis adopts the underlying
principle---evaluate actions by expected benefit, cost and constraints---without
claiming to reproduce that paper's asymptotic method. Reorder and retention are
kept as separate action families because their outcomes, costs, permissions and
uncertainties differ.

The complete framework is an information-system artifact as well as an
analytical study. Design-science research builds and evaluates artifacts in
relation to a relevant problem and a knowledge base \cite{hevner2004design}.
This supports an evaluation plan that includes predictive validity, software
behavior, owner usability and decision consequences instead of reporting model
accuracy as the sole measure of success.

\subsection{Cross-Stream Evidence Synthesis}

No single literature stream supplies the complete argument for the proposed
artifact. Bangladesh adoption studies establish contextual requirements but do
not test transaction-level models. Forecasting and customer analytics provide
task methods but usually begin after an analytical dataset has already been
constructed. Leakage, calibration and explanation research provide safeguards
but not an SME operating workflow. Prescriptive analytics connects estimates
to decisions, while design science requires that the resulting artifact be
evaluated against its intended problem. Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_synthesis}
shows how these bodies of evidence contribute different, non-substitutable
requirements.

\section{Literature-Review Procedure}
\label{sec:literature_review}

The present review is a structured background study, not a claim of an
exhaustive systematic review. Sources were selected for direct relevance to one
of six themes: Bangladesh SME digitalization, BI and design science, customer
analytics, retail demand/inventory, churn and evaluation integrity, and
explainable or prescriptive analytics. Searches used combinations of terms such
as \emph{Bangladesh SME ICT adoption}, \emph{retail demand forecasting},
\emph{intermittent demand}, \emph{RFM customer segmentation}, \emph{churn
profit}, \emph{data leakage}, \emph{probability calibration}, \emph{concept
drift} and \emph{prescriptive analytics}.

Peer-reviewed journal or conference work was preferred. A source was retained
when it defined a required concept, supplied a method or evaluation principle,
or provided Bangladesh-specific evidence. Metadata for every DOI-bearing entry
was checked against Crossref, and dataset records were checked against DataCite
or the official repository. Specific numerical claims were included only when
supported by the paper metadata, abstract or full record available to the
authors. The core set contains 22 scholarly works; three candidate datasets are
listed separately in Chapter~\ref{chapter_methodology}, giving exactly 25 cited
references in this initial report.

\section{Critical Comparative Analysis}

\subsection{Unit of Evidence and Temporal Boundary}

The reviewed studies operate at different units of evidence, and their findings
cannot be combined without respecting those units. An adoption survey may
explain variation in managers' reported intention or organizational readiness;
it does not supply repeated SKU outcomes. A retail forecasting benchmark treats
each series and origin as a prediction case; it does not observe whether a small
business could act on the result. A customer model treats a historical customer
snapshot as a case; it cannot use a later purchase to describe that snapshot.
Design-science evaluation, in turn, treats the artifact in context as the object
of study \cite{hevner2004design}. These are complementary forms of evidence,
not interchangeable sample sizes.

Time is the principal boundary connecting them. For a decision at cutoff $t$,
an operational event is admissible only if it was recorded and available by
$t$; a feature summarizes only admissible events; an outcome occurs after $t$;
and any later correction must be versioned rather than inserted silently into
the original snapshot. This rule is stronger than sorting a completed dataset
by date. It requires the researcher to distinguish event time, recording time
and analytical availability. The leakage taxonomy described by Kaufman et al.
shows why apparently innocuous variables or preprocessing choices can violate
this separation \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

\subsection{Comparator Adequacy and Model Eligibility}

The comparative literature does not support a universal model hierarchy.
Random Forest and XGBoost are strong general-purpose tabular learners
\cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}, but their suitability depends on feature
availability, sample size, drift and the loss attached to error. Intermittent
demand requires comparators designed for sparse occurrence patterns
\cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent}. Large retail
competitions demonstrate the continuing importance of statistical structure,
ensembles and well-constructed baselines \cite{makridakis2022m5}. A credible
experiment must therefore ask whether a candidate is eligible before asking
whether it wins.

Eligibility includes at least four tests. First, the training history must
contain enough independent temporal variation for the candidate's complexity.
Second, all required features must be available at the intended prediction
time. Third, the candidate must produce the output needed by the decision, such
as a calibrated probability or interval rather than only a point ranking.
Fourth, its operational cost and failure behavior must be proportionate to any
measured improvement. A marginal accuracy gain accompanied by unstable
probabilities, opaque failure or infeasible latency is not automatically the
best system choice.

Baseline selection is part of the research hypothesis. Seasonal-naive and
moving-average forecasts test whether learned calendar and context effects add
value beyond recent repetition. Croston-family methods test whether sparse
demand benefits from separate size and interval estimation. A recency rule and
base-rate customer model test whether a classifier adds information beyond the
strongest obvious behavior. Current-owner or reorder-point policies provide the
operational comparator for a forecast-driven action. Without these comparators,
a model can appear successful merely because the task is predictable at all.

\subsection{Metric Alignment and Decision Consequences}

Each metric answers a limited question. MAE summarizes absolute point error;
MASE supports scale-aware comparison; WAPE emphasizes total absolute deviation
relative to observed volume; interval coverage and width describe uncertainty.
None alone measures cash tied in inventory. Similarly, ROC-AUC measures ranking
across thresholds, whereas precision--recall analysis is often more informative
about retrieving a minority outcome \cite{saito2015pr}. The Brier score and
reliability analysis ask whether predicted probabilities are numerically
trustworthy \cite{niculescu2005calibration}. Silhouette values address geometric
cluster separation, not commercial usefulness \cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}.

The evaluation must preserve these distinctions before joining them. A forecast
candidate first needs valid out-of-time predictive evidence. A replenishment
policy then converts the forecast distribution into an order under lead time,
cash, storage and pack-size constraints. Only the second comparison can estimate
holding and shortage consequences, and those estimates remain conditional on
the cost model. The same logic applies to customer analytics: ranking and
calibration precede contact prioritization; contact outcome and incremental
value require additional field evidence.

This layered evaluation guards against metric substitution. It prevents high
ROC-AUC from being reported as a profitable campaign, low WAPE from being
reported as reduced stock-outs, and an internally coherent cluster from being
reported as a stable market segment. It also permits a useful qualified result:
a model may improve one analytical property without improving the subsequent
decision under the current constraints.

\subsection{Contextual Transfer and Evidence Boundaries}

External evidence is necessary for method development but insufficient for a
local effectiveness claim. UCI retail invoices can expose realistic returns,
quantities and transaction irregularities; a public Bangladesh demand series
can exercise a local quantity-forecast procedure; market-price data can provide
external context. Their schemas, units, customers and data-generating processes
are different. Pooling their outcomes into one accuracy number would obscure,
not strengthen, validity.

Bangladesh-specific studies reduce a different uncertainty. They show that
cost, management support, relative advantage, workflow and everyday digital
affordances matter in the local setting
\cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital,hazra2021mfs}. They do not establish the
predictive effect of a festival feature or a reorder policy. The synthesis is
therefore asymmetric: contextual studies inform requirements and evaluation
questions; primary transaction records test local analytical claims; external
datasets test portability or software behavior within an explicit boundary.

Generalization must consequently be decomposed. \emph{Temporal
generalization} concerns later periods in a participating firm. \emph{Entity
generalization} concerns unseen customers, products or branches.
\emph{Organizational generalization} concerns another business, and
\emph{geographical generalization} concerns a wider Bangladesh population.
Success at an earlier level does not establish the next. The planned reporting
will state which level each split and result actually evaluates.

\subsection{Integration Cost and Failure Propagation}

An integrated system creates value by sharing governed operational evidence,
but integration also propagates failure. An incorrect product mapping can
distort a KPI, a lag feature, an inactivity history and an order proposal at the
same time. A late return can affect sales, customer value and inventory. An
unversioned correction can make a previously reported model score impossible to
reproduce. These dependencies explain why the central research gap is not
closed by assembling independently accurate models behind one dashboard.

The appropriate response is controlled coupling. Tasks share canonical events,
identities and provenance, but each analytical view has its own eligibility
rules, cutoff, target and evaluation. A failure in unit cost can block a margin
or profit claim without blocking unit-demand forecasting. Missing customer
identity can block inactivity analysis without invalidating branch-level sales.
This task-level isolation is especially important in SMEs, where demanding a
perfect enterprise dataset before any analysis would make the artifact
impractical.

The review therefore yields six design principles: preserve source evidence;
define decision time before features; use task-specific eligibility; compare
against a meaningful baseline; separate predictive, decision and user claims;
and retain an accountable outcome loop. These principles are not additional
algorithms. They are the logic that makes the later algorithms testable and the
resulting recommendations contestable.

\section{Thematic Synthesis of Prior Work}

\subsection{Business and Bangladesh Evidence}

The BI literature supplies the descriptive--predictive--prescriptive frame
\cite{chen2012business}, while design science supplies an artifact-oriented
research logic \cite{hevner2004design}. Bangladesh studies explain why
organizational support, perceived value, cost, complexity and actual local use
should influence the proposed design \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital,
hazra2021mfs}. Their limitation for the present problem is not quality but unit
of analysis: adoption surveys and interviews do not provide invoice-level
evidence for demand, inactivity or inventory decisions.

\subsection{Customer Evidence}

Fader et al. provide a probabilistic link between transaction history and
future value \cite{fader2005rfm}, while the silhouette method provides a
separate check on whether candidate behavioral clusters are internally coherent
\cite{rousseeuw1987silhouettes}. Together they motivate behavior-based profiles,
but they do not by themselves establish a leakage-safe, non-contractual
inactivity model for Bangladeshi retail.

\subsection{Forecasting and Inventory Evidence}

M5 supplies large, retail-specific comparative evidence
\cite{makridakis2022m5}. Retail review evidence
\cite{fildes2022retail}, intermittent-demand methods
\cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent}, hierarchical
reconciliation \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical} and stock-control evaluation
\cite{syntetos2010stock} show why an SME comparison requires more than an LSTM
and one error column.

\subsection{Risk, Integrity and Action Evidence}

Precision--recall analysis and probability calibration address two different
properties of a risk output
\cite{saito2015pr,niculescu2005calibration}. Leakage research explains why
apparently excellent results can be unusable \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.
SHAP and human-centred explanation research then address inspection and
communication \cite{lundberg2017shap,miller2019explanation}. Finally,
prescriptive analytics motivates
placing predictions inside a cost-and-constraint model
\cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}. The unresolved question is whether these
principles can be joined into a low-resource SME workflow and evaluated on the
same temporal evidence chain.

\section{Research Gap and Thesis Positioning}
\label{sec:research_gap}

The gap is derived by comparing what each literature stream establishes with
what the stated SME decision problem requires. It is deliberately bounded to
the 22-work scholarly core set; the thesis does not claim that no related system exists
anywhere. Six connected gaps emerge.

\paragraph{G1---Contextual and empirical gap.}
Bangladesh research explains determinants and lived affordances of digital
adoption \cite{hoque2016ict,shahadat2023digital,hazra2021mfs}, but it does not
provide a joint, longitudinal test of SKU demand, customer inactivity and
constrained retail actions. Conversely, large retail and forecast studies offer
strong analytical evidence \cite{makridakis2022m5,fildes2022retail}, but their
scale, market and infrastructure do not establish validity for owner-managed
Bangladeshi SMEs. Local adoption evidence and transaction-level analytical
evidence therefore remain disconnected.

\paragraph{G2---Operational-to-analytical integration gap.}
BI and CRM research show how operational data can support multiple analytical
functions \cite{chen2012business}. The forecasting, segmentation and
churn studies in the matrix generally begin from an already prepared analytical
dataset. They do not jointly evaluate the path from an auditable business event,
through a reproducible historical snapshot, to the prediction and later action
outcome. For the target SME, this missing path matters because a model cannot be
reproduced if invoice corrections, stock movements, organization scope and
feature cutoffs are not traceable.

\paragraph{G3---Methodological-validity gap.}
Prior work separately establishes the risks of leakage
\cite{kaufman2012leakage}, intermittent demand
\cite{croston1972intermittent,syntetos2005intermittent}, incoherent hierarchical
forecasts \cite{hyndman2011hierarchical}, unsuitable error measures
\cite{hyndman2006accuracy} and uncalibrated classification probabilities
\cite{niculescu2005calibration}. What is missing is a single SME evaluation
protocol that applies these safeguards together: decision-time feature
availability, temporal/entity-safe splits, baseline eligibility by series type,
coherent aggregation, calibration and an untouched final test.

\paragraph{G4---Prediction-to-decision gap.}
Accurate forecasts or customer rankings are intermediate outputs. Stock-control
and profit-aware churn studies demonstrate that operational consequences can
change model choice \cite{syntetos2010stock}; prescriptive
analytics formalizes the use of predictions inside a decision problem
\cite{bertsimas2020prescriptive}. The unresolved need is a transparent,
low-resource mechanism that converts the outputs of several SME analytical
tasks into actions while checking cash, stock, lead time, order rules, contact
consent, uncertainty and action cost.

\paragraph{G5---Owner-centred explanation gap.}
SHAP provides principled feature attributions for complex predictions
\cite{lundberg2017shap}, while human-explanation research shows
that useful explanations are selective and contextual
\cite{miller2019explanation}. Neither technical attribution alone nor a generic
dashboard establishes that a Bangladeshi SME owner understands the evidence,
distinguishes association from cause, and completes the intended decision
correctly. Explanation quality must therefore be evaluated as owner task
performance, not inferred from the presence of an explanation chart.

\paragraph{G6---Lifecycle-accountability gap.}
Business relationships between inputs and outcomes may change, and design
science requires evaluation of the artifact in relation to its problem
\cite{hevner2004design}. The reviewed task
studies do not collectively close the local loop by recording model/baseline
version, data cutoff, recommendation, owner response, later outcome and
revalidation decision. Without that record, a system cannot show whether a
recommendation remained appropriate after business conditions changed.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Central research gap.} Within the reviewed evidence, there is no jointly
evaluated, Bangladesh-oriented retail SME framework that connects traceable
operational records to leakage-controlled and calibrated multi-task analytics,
then converts those outputs into constraint-aware, owner-understandable actions
whose use and outcomes can be monitored over time.
\end{quote}

The central gap is integrative and evaluative. It does not require inventing a
new classifier; it requires showing that established methods can be combined
without breaking data lineage, temporal validity, decision feasibility or human
accountability. Table~\ref{tab:gap_traceability} makes each gap falsifiable by
linking it to a proposed response and evaluation.

\system{} is positioned as the proposed response to this combined gap. Public or
generated evidence can test parts of the procedure; closing the contextual,
decision and owner-centred gaps requires consenting SME data and participant
evaluation. If those data are unavailable, the final thesis must report the
corresponding gaps as still open rather than treating a prototype or external
benchmark as proof of local effectiveness.
