\section{Motivations}

Business intelligence and analytics convert operational records into
descriptive, predictive and prescriptive support for decision makers
\cite{chen2012business}. The opportunity is particularly relevant to small
businesses because a modest shop already generates invoices, product movements,
mobile-wallet payments, customer histories and supplier transactions. The data
often remain separated across paper ledgers, spreadsheets and point solutions,
so the owner can answer what happened but not reliably why it happened or what
action should follow.

Consider an inventory-based retailer before Eid. The owner must decide how much
stock to purchase, which customers are becoming inactive, which product or
branch is underperforming and whether a promotion is profitable. Under-forecasting
causes stock-outs and lost sales; over-forecasting locks scarce capital in slow
stock. A generic monthly chart cannot resolve this decision because it does not
combine demand history, current stock, supplier lead time, promotion, calendar
context, uncertainty and cash constraints.

Bangladesh adds important design requirements. Research on rural Bangladeshi
SMEs found that organizational, technological and environmental factors shape
ICT adoption \cite{hoque2016ict}, while later work identifies cost, management
support, relative advantage, competitive pressure and government support as
significant adoption determinants \cite{shahadat2023digital}. Studies of mobile
payment and MFS use also show that ease, expected benefit and local usage
practices matter \cite{rahman2023mobile,hazra2021mfs}. Therefore, a usable
system should treat local payment channels and business calendars as core data,
use low-friction workflows, and explain recommendations in language an owner can
act upon.

The approved thesis title is broad, but the implementation scope is deliberately
narrowed to Bangladeshi retail and inventory-based SMEs. This makes data
requirements, workflows, evaluation targets and business actions concrete while
allowing future sector-specific templates.

\section{Problem Statement}

Existing small-business tools commonly stop at transaction recording and static
dashboards. Standalone research models, on the other hand, may forecast or
classify a public dataset without entering the operational loop where a business
records sales, controls stock and acts on a prediction. Three gaps follow:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Fragmented operations and analytics.} Sales, inventory,
  customer, purchase and payment data are not maintained in one auditable,
  tenant-isolated system.
  \item \textbf{Weak scientific validity.} Synthetic or foreign data are
  sometimes treated as local field evidence; repeated customers cross random
  train/test splits; post-outcome variables leak into features; and complex
  models are not compared against simple baselines.
  \item \textbf{No decision bridge.} A model score is presented without a
  feasible action, uncertainty, business constraint, local explanation or
  mechanism for recording outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

The central problem is consequently to design and evaluate one reproducible
framework that connects trustworthy data capture, leakage-free analytics and
actionable strategy for Bangladeshi SMEs. The work must remain useful on small
datasets, must disclose when a baseline is stronger than an AI model, and must
not claim production readiness before real local validation.

\section{Objectives}

\subsection{General Objective}

The general objective is to develop and evaluate an AI-powered business
analytics system that helps Bangladeshi retail SMEs monitor performance,
extract explainable insights and prioritize feasible business actions.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}

\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*.]
  \item Design a canonical, auditable and tenant-isolated data model covering
  sales, stock, purchases, returns, customers, suppliers, payments and expenses.
  \item Build reproducible preprocessing and temporal feature pipelines for
  demand, future inactivity, segmentation and performance analysis.
  \item Compare statistical, machine-learning and simple baseline methods using
  task-appropriate metrics and leakage-safe splits.
  \item provide global and instance-level explanations while clearly indicating
  whether a recommendation comes from a trained model or a baseline rule.
  \item Convert forecasts, risks and KPIs into constraint-aware reorder,
  retention and operational action rankings.
  \item Validate the method first on transparent synthetic/public datasets and
  finally on consenting Bangladeshi SME records and owner-centered evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}

The contribution is an integrated framework rather than a claim of a new base
classifier. At the present stage, the project contributes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{An operational-to-analytical architecture.} The same local
  system records business events, exports a canonical research dataset, trains
  models and serves recommendations.
  \item \textbf{Scientific guardrails.} Customer-level or temporal splits,
  training-only transformations, explicit baseline comparisons, provenance and
  separation of synthetic, public-real and primary-real evidence are part of the
  design.
  \item \textbf{A documented leakage case study.} A legacy 94.69\% segment score
  used target-defining CLV. A new development-only tuning protocol and untouched
  1,000-customer test produce 69.90\% selected-model accuracy; a post-purchase
  satisfaction feature similarly inflated return prediction.
  \item \textbf{Bangladesh-aware product design.} The schema supports BDT,
  bKash, Nagad, Bangla QR, local seasonal events and a Bangla-first operational
  interface.
  \item \textbf{A constraint-aware recommendation layer.} Demand, stock, lead
  time, consent and cost are evaluated before an action is ranked.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible real-data evidence.} The repository contains a
  common daily-demand benchmark on UCI and Bangladesh observations, a UCI
  future-repeat study, WFP/DAM price benchmarks, saved models, audit JSON,
  figures and tests.
\end{itemize}

The intended final contributions additionally include a consenting multi-SME
dataset protocol, leave-one-business-out evaluation, calibrated uncertainty,
owner usability results and a simulated or pilot business-impact comparison.

\section{Challenges}

The principal challenge is data rather than model availability. Public
Bangladeshi sources currently provide market prices or a single demand series,
but not the joint customer--SKU--stock--transaction history required for the
full research questions. Partner recruitment, consent, de-identification and
schema reconciliation are therefore on the critical path.

Technical challenges include intermittent SKU demand, few observations for new
businesses, changing festivals and prices, missing identifiers, class imbalance,
concept drift and the gap between an accurate prediction and a profitable
decision. Scientific challenges include leakage, fair baseline selection,
uncertainty calibration and transportability from one business to another.
Human challenges include digital skill variation, time pressure at the point of
sale, trust in model output and explaining uncertainty without technical jargon.
